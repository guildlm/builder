NOTE — read this BEFORE the v4-vs-v5 diff: up to a third of the rows go DEAD for a reason
that has nothing to do with defence
==========================================================================================

Written 10:45 on 29 July, before hole_hunt has swept anything, so it cannot be a
rationalisation of a number I have already seen.

THE MECHANISM
_hole_hunt cannot grade a tree that does not pass its own tests. Every probe on a red
artifact returns BASELINE-RED, and the tool says so plainly:

    0 of 1 probes could answer · 1 BASELINE-RED (artifact already failing: expreval-v5)
    NOTHING WAS MEASURED — every probe hit a red baseline or failed to apply.
    This is not 'no holes found'.

That is correct behaviour and it is exactly what makes the diff misreadable.

THE ARITHMETIC, from the tracked baseline

    taskapi-v4       40 rows      taskapi-v5   shipped NOT-GREEN
    expreval-v4       8 rows      expreval-v5  shipped NOT-GREEN
                     -------
                     48 of 314 baseline rows = 15%, already certain

    taskapipro-v4    62 rows      taskapipro-v5 is at fix round 5 of 6 and has not compiled
                     -------      once; NOT-GREEN is the likely outcome
                    110 of 314 = 35% if it ships red

So somewhere between a seventh and a third of the comparable set will show a LIVE verdict in
v4 and a DEAD one in v5. Not one of those rows is a defence that weakened. They are trees
that stopped compiling.

WHAT THE DIFF WILL DO WITH THEM, AND WHY IT IS NOT ENOUGH
_redraw_diff already separates this. It counts DEAD->LIVE and LIVE->DEAD on their own line
and labels them "artifacts changing state, not defences changing", and a NOAPPLY ->
BASELINE-RED flip is excluded from the live count entirely. The tool is not going to lie.

The risk is the HEADLINE. "N of M comparable rows flipped" invites reading M as the corpus,
and a third of M being dead means the campaign's closing number rests on a much smaller
base than it appears to. The count that matters is:

    rows LIVE in BOTH sweeps — the only ones where CAUGHT -> SURVIVED can even be observed.

Report that first, before any flip count, and state the three red artifacts by name.

AND THE DIRECTION MATTERS MORE THAN THE COUNT
taskapi's 40 rows going dead is not a neutral loss. taskapi-v5 is red BECAUSE the empty-list
closure fired against a real nil-slice defect — the defence working exactly as designed. So
those 40 rows are unmeasurable in v5 as a DIRECT CONSEQUENCE of a closure succeeding. A
naive reading — "40 rows we can no longer grade" — inverts a success into an absence.

    The one artifact where a closure caught a genuine bug is the one that contributes
    nothing to the capstone comparison, because catching the bug is what made it red.

That is worth stating as a limitation of mutation sweeps generally: they can only measure
suites that pass, so a suite that is currently DOING ITS JOB is invisible to them.

⚠️ DO NOT "FIX" THIS BY REPAIRING THE RED TREES. Hand-patching taskapi-v5 to green would
make its rows gradeable and would destroy the thing they would be graded on — the defect is
what the closure caught. If a comparable number for taskapi is wanted it needs a fresh draw,
not a repair.


COHERENCE CHECK (11:12) — THE v5 CORPUS MATCHES THE CURRENT SPECS, WITH ONE KNOWN EXCEPTION
============================================================================================
The capstone asks whether the REPAIRED SPEC SET produces a better corpus. That question is
only answerable if the corpus was actually drawn from the specs as they now stand. If specs
had been edited mid-sweep the v5 trees would be a mix of before-and-after and the comparison
would be about nothing in particular.

Checked by comparing every spec's mtime against its tree's:

    22 v5 trees with a spec · 1 has a spec NEWER than the draw

    taskflow      spec edited 07-29 00:42     tree drawn 07-29 00:39     SPEC-NEWER

That is the rename, by three minutes, and it is the case already known and already queued
for a retest. Every other tree was drawn from the spec as it stands today.

So the corpus is spec-coherent, and the one incoherent artifact is the one whose incoherence
IS the pending experiment. _named_present flags it by itself, which is why that hint was
added.

⚠️ MTIME IS FORGEABLE AND IS A HINT, NOT A PROOF — the same caveat _named_present carries.
It is evidence that no spec was quietly edited mid-sweep, not a guarantee. The stronger check
is `git log --since` over specs/, and for the three specs that matter most the answer is
already known from their commits: taskapipro's paging repair and shortener's prohibition both
predate the sweep, and taskflow's rename is the exception above.

⚠️ AND IT SAYS NOTHING ABOUT ledger, workapi OR THE taskapipro REDRAW, which are not drawn
yet. Re-run this check after the corpus completes; a spec edited between now and then would
be invisible to the run above.
