    On process B the PLACEBO flips the file. That arm renames nothing, mentions no sentinel, and

⚠️⚠️⚠️ AND IT INDICTS AN EARLIER DESIGN OF MINE — THE ONE THAT SHIPPED
    6 August's result ("showing the declaration form is the whole treatment; the other two changes
    are decoration", 386f2f8) rests on arms of exactly this shape:

        base / base2   old spec        ABSENT, byte-identical
        treated/×2     new spec        LONG,   byte-identical
        varonly        component B     LONG

    THERE IS NO PLACEBO ANYWHERE IN THAT SERIES. No arm edits the spec by a comparable amount
    WITHOUT the sentinel change. On a process of type B, every one of those arms would have come
    out exactly as it did — and the conclusion "showing the declaration form is what repairs it"
    would have been indistinguishable from "editing models.go's purpose at all flips this
    process". The unbundling has the same hole: on a type-B process, `varonly` flipping proves
    nothing about component B, because ANY edit flips it.

    That is not a retraction. It is an untested confound in a published result, found by a control
    that a later experiment happened to include. Two of the three processes probed today for a
    treated arm (71409 sensitive; 60986 insensitive) means the confound is not exotic.

📌 THE METHOD CHANGE THIS BUYS — A PLACEBO SCREEN BEFORE ANY TREATMENT ARM
    A process must EARN the right to host a treatment: probe it, then run a content-free edit of
    comparable size in the SAME purpose the treatment will edit. If the placebo flips, the process
    cannot discriminate and must be discarded like a LONG baseline is discarded — recorded in the
    ledger, not quietly re-probed. Only a process that is informative AND placebo-null can carry a
    treatment arm. Today's design got this right by accident, for the store purposes; every
    sentinel experiment since 5 August got it wrong by omission.
