FINDING — the order fix has a COST, and it is the stub's own doing
=================================================================
30 July, 23:20. Found by checking, immediately after promoting the fix, whether the fix has a price.
It does.

THE NUMBERS
    ledger p6   A green (fix round 3)   ·  B green (fix round 2)
    ledger p7   A green (fix round 2)   ·  B EXHAUSTED 6 rounds, still failing
    ledger p8   A green (fix round 3)   ·  B EXHAUSTED 6 rounds, still failing
    workapi w1  A EXHAUSTED 7 rounds    ·  B running

    ledger: A (original order, WITH the stub) converged 3 of 3. B (swapped, correct placement)
    converged 1 of 3.

WHY THIS IS NOT A REASON TO REVERT — and why it is still a real cost
The 29 July finding said it in one line: "a stub compiles, vets and tests clean." That is exactly
why A converges easily. With memory.go at fourteen bytes there is no second implementation to
reconcile — store.go holds everything, nothing contradicts it, and the fix loop has almost nothing
to repair. The tree is green because half the work was never done.

    THE FIX DOES NOT CREATE THE DIFFICULTY. IT REVEALS IT. B has real code in two files that must
    agree, which is the project the spec actually describes, and reconciling them is work the
    stubbed tree simply skipped.

    So the trade is:
        BEFORE  green, and silently shipping an empty implementation
        AFTER   correctly placed, and sometimes honestly failing to converge

    That is the better trade — a visible failure beats a silent one — but it IS a trade, and a
    promotion that did not carry it would have been a half-truth.

⚠️ n = 3 ledger pairs. 3-of-3 versus 1-of-3 is suggestive, not established, and the direction could
soften with more pairs. It is recorded now because it was found now, and because the alternative is
discovering it later as a regression in convergence rate that nobody attributed to this change.

⚠️ AND IT REFRAMES THE 58% CORPUS RATE. Those trees were largely GREEN. Every green stubbed tree in
the corpus is a project that reported success while shipping a fourteen-byte file where the spec
describes an eight-method store. The convergence statistics of this pipeline, over the whole month,
are inflated by exactly the defect the fix removes.

    That is a bigger claim than the fix itself and it is stated as an implication, not a measurement:
    I have not gone back and scored what fraction of green corpus trees carry a stub. That is a free
    analysis and it is the obvious next one.

RECOMMENDATION UNCHANGED, CAVEAT ATTACHED
Keep the reorder. Record that convergence may drop, that the drop is the spec's real difficulty
surfacing rather than new breakage, and that fix-round budgets may need raising for these specs —
5 budgeted was enough when a third of the work was being skipped.
