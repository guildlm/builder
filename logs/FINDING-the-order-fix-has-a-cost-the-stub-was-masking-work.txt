FINDING — the order fix has a COST, and it is the stub's own doing
=================================================================
30 July, 23:20. Found by checking, immediately after promoting the fix, whether the fix has a price.
It does.

THE NUMBERS
    ledger p6   A green (fix round 3)   ·  B green (fix round 2)
    ledger p7   A green (fix round 2)   ·  B EXHAUSTED 6 rounds, still failing
    ledger p8   A green (fix round 3)   ·  B EXHAUSTED 6 rounds, still failing
    workapi w1  A EXHAUSTED 7 rounds    ·  B running

    ledger: A (original order, WITH the stub) converged 3 of 3. B (swapped, correct placement)
    converged 1 of 3.

WHY THIS IS NOT A REASON TO REVERT — and why it is still a real cost
The 29 July finding said it in one line: "a stub compiles, vets and tests clean." That is exactly
why A converges easily. With memory.go at fourteen bytes there is no second implementation to
reconcile — store.go holds everything, nothing contradicts it, and the fix loop has almost nothing
to repair. The tree is green because half the work was never done.

    THE FIX DOES NOT CREATE THE DIFFICULTY. IT REVEALS IT. B has real code in two files that must
    agree, which is the project the spec actually describes, and reconciling them is work the
    stubbed tree simply skipped.

    So the trade is:
        BEFORE  green, and silently shipping an empty implementation
        AFTER   correctly placed, and sometimes honestly failing to converge

    That is the better trade — a visible failure beats a silent one — but it IS a trade, and a
    promotion that did not carry it would have been a half-truth.

⚠️ n = 3 ledger pairs. 3-of-3 versus 1-of-3 is suggestive, not established, and the direction could
soften with more pairs. It is recorded now because it was found now, and because the alternative is
discovering it later as a regression in convergence rate that nobody attributed to this change.

⚠️ AND IT REFRAMES THE 58% CORPUS RATE. Those trees were largely GREEN. Every green stubbed tree in
the corpus is a project that reported success while shipping a fourteen-byte file where the spec
describes an eight-method store. The convergence statistics of this pipeline, over the whole month,
are inflated by exactly the defect the fix removes.

    That is a bigger claim than the fix itself and it is stated as an implication, not a measurement:
    I have not gone back and scored what fraction of green corpus trees carry a stub. That is a free
    analysis and it is the obvious next one.

RECOMMENDATION UNCHANGED, CAVEAT ATTACHED
Keep the reorder. Record that convergence may drop, that the drop is the spec's real difficulty
surfacing rather than new breakage, and that fix-round budgets may need raising for these specs —
5 budgeted was enough when a third of the work was being skipped.

MEASURED, 23:35 — "green while shipping a stub" is REAL, at n=5 with direct logs
The 23:20 note said the corpus implication was stated but unmeasured. Measured now, two ways.

    CORPUS PROXY — inconclusive, and the proxy is the reason
    Using the '_fail-' filename prefix as a stand-in for a failed draw:
        among _fail- trees   13 of 21 stubbed (62%)
        among the others     48 of 97 stubbed (49%)
    So stubbing is somewhat commoner among recorded failures, and roughly HALF of everything not
    named _fail- is stubbed. ⚠️ But '_fail-' is a naming convention from particular experiments, not
    a systematic green/not-green record. This denominator is "trees nobody labelled a failure", not
    "green trees". It is consistent with the implication and does not establish it.

    DIRECT — today's stubbed draws, from their own logs
        ledger-pairA-p6   memory.go 14   converged to GREEN after fix round 3
        ledger-pairA-p7   memory.go 14   converged to GREEN after fix round 2
        ledger-pairA-p8   memory.go 14   converged to GREEN after fix round 3
        ledger-prose2     memory.go 14   exhausted 6 fix rounds
        workapi-pairA-w1  memory.go 14   exhausted 7 fix rounds

    THREE OF FIVE STUBBED DRAWS WENT GREEN. Each is a project that reported success while shipping
    fourteen bytes where the spec describes a full store — and each did it in two or three fix
    rounds, i.e. easily.

    That settles the direction the proxy could not: a stub does not merely SURVIVE the pipeline, it
    passes it comfortably, which is what "compiles, vets and tests clean" predicted and nobody had
    checked against a green count.

⚠️ WHAT IS STILL NOT MEASURED: the fraction of the 107-tree CORPUS that is green-and-stubbed. Old
trees carry no logs, and re-running builds to find out is real CPU against a claim that is already
directionally settled at n=5. Recorded as the boundary of the claim, not as a gap to be quietly
ignored: today's draws show green-and-stubbed happens and happens easily; the month-long rate is
unquantified.

UPDATE, 00:10 — workapi's pair is a TIE, which softens the cost signal
    ledger  p6   A green (3)   B green (2)
    ledger  p7   A green (2)   B exhausted
    ledger  p8   A green (3)   B exhausted
    workapi w1   A exhausted   B exhausted        <- BOTH fail; the reorder changed placement
                                                     without changing the outcome

    Totals across all four pairs: A green 3 of 4 · B green 1 of 4.

    ⚠️ THE COST IS LEDGER-SHAPED, NOT UNIVERSAL, on this evidence. workapi failed identically either
    way — its hard spot is internal/service/service_test.go, which consumed A's entire 7-round budget
    and B's entire 6-round budget alike. So on the one spec where convergence was already the
    binding problem, the reorder cost nothing.

    The 23:20 reading stands but narrows: where a stub was making a tree LOOK easy, removing it
    surfaces real work (ledger, 3-of-3 down to 1-of-3). Where the tree was already failing for
    unrelated reasons, the reorder is free (workapi, 0-of-1 both ways).

    ⚠️ n is 4 pairs. Both statements are directional.
