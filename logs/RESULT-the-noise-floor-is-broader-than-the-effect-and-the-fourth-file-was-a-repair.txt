A CONFOUND I RAISED AGAINST THE XPROC DESIGN, AND WHY IT DOES NOT BITE
In `_parity_xproc.sh` every pair restarts the server, then draws ctl FIRST and arm SECOND. So
within every pair, control = cold process, arm = warmed — perfectly confounded, and warmth is a
variable this campaign already took seriously (PREREG-warmth-versus-process-identity, 30 July).

The in-process run breaks it. `parity-ab-run.log` shows one process (pid 7598) alternating:
    ctl-1 (draw 1) · arm-1 (2) · ctl-2 (3) · arm-2 (4) · ctl-3 (5) · arm-3 (6)
ctl-3 is the FIFTH draw in that process — warmer than arm-1, the second — and it is SPLIT. Warmth
is fully crossed with condition there and the verdict tracks CONDITION, 3/3 against 3/3. The xproc
pairs cannot separate the two on their own; they do not need to.

⚠️ The noise-floor numbers above ARE warmth-matched by construction: ctl-1/ctl-2 are both
first-after-restart, arm-1/arm-2 both second. That was luck, not design — the runner's fixed order
happens to make the two cross-process comparisons clean while making the within-pair one dirty.
