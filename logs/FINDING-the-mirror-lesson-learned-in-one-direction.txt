WHY THIS ONE IS WORTH WRITING DOWN

Not because four undefended branches is a big hole — it is a small one, and each branch is
four lines. It is worth writing down because of where the spec already says the right thing.
The entry directly above, describing the SUCCESS path, reads:

    "The three duration vars are structural MIRRORS of one another — exercise all three, not
     just the first; a mirror nobody exercises is a branch nobody has shown to work."

That sentence is in this spec because the campaign learned the mirror lesson and I wrote it
in. It is attached to TestLoadFromEnv, which reads all six values on the happy path. The
ERROR path — the same vars, the same mirror, the same argument word for word — got
TestLoadInvalidDuration, singular, for one variable.

    The lesson was learned in one direction and not carried to the other, in the same entry,
    by the person who wrote the sentence.

That is the same shape as every mirror this campaign has found — Delete's 404 against Get's,
projects' clamp against tasks', one collection's empty list against the other's — except
this time both halves are in one file, one entry, four inches apart. Proximity did not help.
Nor did having the general rule written down: the rule was applied to the case in front of
me and not to its twin.

WHAT WOULD CLOSE IT

One test, own-named, that sets a NON-DURATION var to garbage — TestLoadInvalidPageSize with
DEFAULT_PAGE_SIZE="abc" — plus a sentence saying the parse-error branches are mirrors of one
another exactly as the success-path vars are. Not five tests: the point of the corpus's own
finding about mirrors is that the SECOND one is what proves the first was not luck, and a
table over all five would collapse back into one function with one assertion.
