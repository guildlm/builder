FINDING — a build whose tests serve requests can never repeat an error surface, by construction
================================================================================================
31 July. Found by predicting that the two arm draws would be byte-identical (the pipeline is
deterministic within a process) and testing it.

THE PREDICTION FAILED, AT LINE 39
    arm-1: error surface ba9e8bac
    arm-2: error surface 165d2d35

    Same spec, same flags, same server process, BYTE-IDENTICAL source trees — and different error
    surfaces on the first fix round.

WHY: `_error_signature` MISSES THE PROGRAM'S OWN LOG OUTPUT
    _SIG_NOISE_RE strips 0x addresses, `goroutine N`, `\d+\.\d+s` test times and `+0x` frame offsets.
    Verified against a real line from the run:

        raw        2026/07/31 13:37:35 GET /accounts -> 200 (1.708µs)
        normalised 2026/07/31 13:37:35 GET /accounts -> 200 (1.708µs)   ← unchanged

    The wall-clock timestamp survives, and so does the µs duration: `\d+\.\d+s` matches neither
    `1.708µs` (the unit is µs) nor `834ns` (no decimal point).

    So once a build's tests actually SERVE REQUESTS, every check embeds a fresh timestamp and the
    surface hash is unique BY CONSTRUCTION. `surface seen before` can never fire, and the extension
    rounds that flag exists to stop are always granted to the cap.

    arm-1: eight surfaces, all distinct, three extension rounds each labelled "new error surface",
    ending in "exhausted 8 fix rounds (5 budgeted), still failing". Not one repeat detected.

THE COST, measured rather than asserted
    654 builds · 9 contain app-log lines · 5 of those ran extension rounds · 0 of those 5 ever logged
    "seen before" · 15 extension rounds granted inside them.

    For contrast, 5 builds WITHOUT app-log lines also ran extension rounds — and those are the ones
    where the repeat detector can work at all.

    ⚠️ 15 rounds is the ceiling on what a fix would have saved, not the saving. Some of those rounds
    would have been granted anyway on genuinely new surfaces. The honest claim is that in 5 builds
    the repeat check was INCAPABLE of firing, not that 15 rounds were wasted.

THE SAME BLIND SPOT, TWICE, INDEPENDENTLY
    `_error_signature` handles heap addresses, goroutine ids and test wall times — and misses
    timestamps and sub-second durations. `_error_keys`, which I wrote TODAY, handled exactly the same
    three classes and missed exactly the same two; that cost four false refusals this morning and was
    fixed in two attempts.

    Two functions, written months apart by different reasoning, converged on the same incomplete list
    of "things that vary between runs". That is not a coincidence — it is what happens when the list
    is assembled from the failures one has already seen.

FIX (queued, not applied — builder.py is frozen until the parity pairs finish)
    Add to _SIG_NOISE_RE: `\d{4}/\d{2}/\d{2} \d{2}:\d{2}:\d{2}` and `\d+(\.\d+)?(ns|µs|ms)`.
    Reject condition: two genuinely different failures must still hash differently — the detector's
    whole job is telling a repeat from a new stratum, and a normalisation that erased too much would
    stop extension rounds that deserve to run.

VALIDATED BEFORE THE EDIT, without touching the frozen file
    Proposed: _SIG_NOISE_RE gains  \d{4}/\d{2}/\d{2} \d{2}:\d{2}:\d{2}  and  \d+(\.\d+)?(ns|µs|ms)

    case                                   normalised now   normalised by proposal
    app log "GET /accounts -> 200 (1.708µs)"     no                yes    <- the defect
    app log "POST /accounts -> 201 (8µs)"        no                yes
    "internal/api/x.go:12:5: undefined: Foo"     no                no     <- must not move
    "--- FAIL: TestGet (0.00s)"                  yes               yes    <- already handled
    "panic: assignment to entry in nil map"      no                no     <- must not move

    AND THE TWO PROPERTIES THAT DECIDE IT:
        same request, different timestamp AND different duration  ->  hashes EQUAL      ✓
        different request (POST/201 vs GET/200)                   ->  hashes DIFFER     ✓

    The second is the pre-registered reject condition: the detector exists to tell a repeat from a new
    stratum, so a normalisation that erased too much would stop extension rounds that deserve to run.
    It survives.

    ⚠️ Validated by evaluating the regex, NOT by running the harness — builder.py stays frozen until
    the parity pairs finish. What this does not test is whether the surrounding call site behaves as
    expected once the hashes start colliding, which needs a build and is the first thing to check
    after the edit lands.

ROUND-LEVEL EVIDENCE from arm-1, which pins exactly when the blindness starts
    round            app-log lines   surface
    fix 1/5                0         a19c144f     <- failed at COMPILE, tests never ran
    fix 2/5                4         70a310da     <- from here on, tests serve requests
    fix 3/5                4         95d6749f
    fix 4/5                4         4c864f3c
    fix 5/5                4         7d644cfa
    extension 6            4         971c719a
    extension 7            4         f16f76f8
    extension 8            5         (log ends)

    Exactly ONE round was compile-only. From round 2 onward every check emitted four app-log lines,
    and every surface after that is distinct — seven consecutive rounds, no repeat possible.

    SO THE 3% ESCAPE HATCH IN THE PREDICTION IS NARROWER THAN I WROTE. A compile-only round does
    occur — round 1 was one — but a DETECTABLE repeat needs TWO of them carrying the same errors.
    One compile-only round has nothing to be compared against.

⚠️ AND THE STRUCTURAL IRONY, which is the part worth remembering
    A build only stays comparable while it is failing to COMPILE. Once it compiles well enough to run
    its tests, the detector goes blind. But a build that never compiles fails fast and does not need
    extension rounds; a build that compiles and then fails its tests is exactly the one that grinds
    through the budget.

    The repeat detector works where it is least needed and is blind where it would pay. That is not a
    tuning problem — it follows from what output each stage produces, and it is why the fix is a
    normalisation rather than a threshold.

THIRD INSTANCE — arm-3, and the pattern is exact across all three arms
    arm-1   surfaces  8 · distinct  8 · app-log lines 30 · "seen before" 0
    arm-2   surfaces  8 · distinct  8 · app-log lines 30 · "seen before" 0
    arm-3   surfaces  2 · distinct  2 · app-log lines  4 · "seen before" 0   (still running)

    Every surface distinct, in every arm, with zero repeats detected. arm-1 and arm-2 are the same
    draw twice so they agree trivially; arm-3 is the same draw a third time and agrees again.

    ⚠️ WHICH MEANS THIS IS STILL ONE OBSERVATION, NOT THREE. All three arms are byte-identical as
    drawn (measured with `_asdrawn_diff.py`), so they cannot disagree about anything the fix loop
    does deterministically. What three identical draws DO establish is that the count of distinct
    surfaces is not luck: the same input produces 8 distinct surfaces every time, and the repeat
    detector fires zero times every time.

    The claim that generalises is structural rather than statistical — a timestamped line in the
    output makes any two surfaces differ, and ledger's spec requires latency logging. Nothing in
    these three runs adds independent evidence for that; the archive measurement (654 builds, 9 with
    app-log lines, 5 of those running extension rounds, 0 ever logging "seen before") is what does.

VALIDATED ON REAL ARCHIVED OUTPUT, not synthetic lines
    Taking arm-1's eight rounds of captured toolchain output (the `!`-prefixed subset the log keeps)
    and signing each with the current regex and with the proposal:

        distinct signatures NOW       8 of 8
        distinct signatures PROPOSED  3 of 8
        -> 5 previously-"new" surfaces collapse onto an earlier one

    So on this build the repeat detector would have fired instead of granting extension rounds. Five
    of the eight rounds were re-examining a surface it had already seen and could not recognise.

    ⚠️ AND 3 OF 8 IS THE RIGHT SHAPE, NOT 1 OF 8. If the proposal collapsed everything to a single
    signature it would have erased the signal along with the noise — the build genuinely passed
    through three distinct error strata, and the fix leaves those three distinguishable. That is the
    pre-registered reject condition ("two genuinely different failures must still hash differently")
    tested against real data rather than a fixture.

    ⚠️ WHAT IT STILL DOES NOT SHOW: whether stopping earlier would have produced a BETTER outcome.
    The build failed after 8 rounds; stopping at round 3 or 4 would have failed sooner and cheaper,
    not converged. The saving is GPU, not green builds, and the earlier estimate of ~20 minutes per
    affected build stands as the honest value.

    AND EVERY SURFACE HASH DIFFERS. Same code, same errors, same number of log lines — different

    ⚠️ THIS IS THE MECHANISM WITH NOTHING ELSE MOVING. The two draws are identical as drawn (measured
    with `_asdrawn_diff.py`), take identical fix trajectories, and emit identical counts of everything

    on the normalisation regex.

    That is the strongest form the evidence takes, and it is worth noting that it only became
    available because a redundant third pair was drawn. The design error that cost 3.5 hours produced
    exactly one thing I could not have got otherwise, and this is it.

PRE-FLIGHT OF THE QUEUED EDIT — all four target points located in the current module
    _SIG_NOISE_RE definition            line 5392
    _ADDR_RE / _GOROUTINE_ID_RE         line 1622
    _canonical_error_line body          line 1626
    _canonical_toolchain_output return  line 1283

    and the three consumers are distinct, live call sites:
        _error_signature            referenced 2x
        _error_entries              referenced 3x   (feeds _error_keys and _error_counts)
        _canonical_toolchain_output referenced 3x

    So the edit is three additions in two regions — the shared normaliser near line 1622 and the
    signature's own regex at 5392 — and nothing has moved under it during the eight code changes made
    today. Checked now rather than at apply time because a queued edit whose landmarks have shifted
    is how a careful change becomes a hurried one.

    ⚠️ AND THE TWO REGIONS ARE NOT ONE. `_canonical_error_line` (1626) serves the repair gate;
    `_SIG_NOISE_RE` (5392) serves the surface hash; `_canonical_toolchain_output` (1283) serves the
    fix prompt. They were written separately, they missed the same two token classes separately, and
    they must each be fixed separately — a single shared normaliser would be the better design and is
    NOT what is queued, because redesigning three consumers is a larger change than fixing them.

ALL THREE ARMS, COMPLETE — identical structure, zero repeats detected, every time

    Three draws, byte-identical as drawn, each producing eight distinct surfaces and each granted all

    zero times in twenty-four opportunities.

    ⚠️ AND IT IS STILL ONE OBSERVATION. The three arms cannot disagree — they are the same input.
    What the triple gives is that the count is exact and stable, not that the phenomenon is common.
    The generalising evidence remains the archive sweep: 654 builds, 9 with app-log lines, 5 of those
    running extension rounds, 0 ever logging a repeat.

    THE SHAPE OF THE WHOLE FINDING, in one line: a spec that asks for latency logging makes its
    build's error surfaces unique by construction, so the extension-round budget is always spent in
    full — and the fix is three normalisations, validated on this build's own output at 8 distinct
    surfaces collapsing to 3.
