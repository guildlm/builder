WHAT 31 JULY LEFT OPEN
    The whole corpus defect is ONE identifier — ErrInsufficientFunds, 9/27 (33%), against five
    siblings at 100% over 13 to 27 trees each. Four properties are unique to it: longest (20
    chars), only two-concept compound, only one with a plausible shorter form, only one whose
    domain has its own package. With n=1 those are four labels on one observation, so the log
    recorded the localisation and refused to name a cause. That has stood since.

    Every one of those four is a property OF THE NAME. That is why none of them can be tested:
    you cannot vary "is the longest" without writing a different identifier, and then the arm
    confounds the property with the substitution.

THE FIFTH PROPERTY, WHICH NOBODY HAD COUNTED — AND IT IS A PROPERTY OF THE SPEC
    How many times does the spec say the name, and in what capacity? Three capacities exist and
    pooling them is what hid this:

        DEMANDED   a described use site: some function is said to return / wrap / assert it
        LISTED     a declaration list, or a status-code mapping, and nowhere else
        FORBIDDEN  named in order to BAN it

        every (project, name) the specs ASK FOR, by described use sites:

            demands mentions  name                   project    declared
                  0        2  ErrInsufficientFunds   ledger       9/27    33%   <-- the defect
                  1        2  ErrNotFound            taskflow    13/13   100%
                  1        3  ErrExists              taskflow    13/13   100%
                  1        5  ErrExists              workapi      2/2    100%
                  1        7  ErrExists              taskapipro   3/3    100%
                  1       12  ErrNotFound            taskapi      3/3    100%
                  1       12  ErrNotFound            workapi      2/2    100%

                  ...  (nine more rows, every one of them 100%)
                  7       10  ErrNotFound            ledger      27/27   100%

    SIXTEEN OF SEVENTEEN HAVE AT LEAST ONE DESCRIBED USE SITE AND ARE DECLARED EVERY TIME. The
    seventeenth has none, and is declared a third of the time.

⚠️ THE SIMPLER VERSION IS REFUTED, FOR FREE, BEFORE ANY DRAW
    "The spec barely mentions it" is NOT the discriminator. taskflow's ErrNotFound is mentioned
    exactly TWICE, in exactly TWO files — the same scarcity as ErrInsufficientFunds — and it is
    declared 13 of 13. The two differ only in the KIND of the second mention: taskflow says
    "Get/Delete return ErrNotFound" (a job), ledger says "ErrInsufficientFunds -> 422" (a table
    row). Raw mention count is dead; capacity survives.

    That is the whole reason this measurement was worth building rather than eyeballing: the
    refutation and the surviving hypothesis differ by one column, and I would not have separated
    them by reading the specs.

⭐ AND IN LEDGER THE USE SITE IS NOT MERELY ABSENT — IT IS EXPLICITLY FORBIDDEN
    specs/ledger-origorder-baseline.yaml, service.go's purpose, lines 296-299:

        "(note: there is NO balance/overdraft check — a balanced transaction always posts as
         long as its accounts exist; do not reject a posting for driving a balance negative,
         because in double-entry one side of every balanced transaction is negative)"

    The one rule that could ever raise an insufficient-funds error is prohibited, at length, in
    the same spec that asks for the error to be declared. The model is told to declare a sentinel
    for a behaviour it is told not to implement. Of the six names, this is the only one in that
    position, and it is the only one that fails.

⚠️⚠️ A CORRECTION I MADE TO MY OWN INSTRUMENT, BEFORE PUBLISHING, NOT AFTER
    The first version of this tool printed:

        mentions 1  ErrNotImplemented  taskapipro  0/3  0%  <-- SCARCE AND DEFECTIVE
        mentions 1  ErrNotImplemented  workapi     0/2  0%  <-- SCARCE AND DEFECTIVE

    and I was one step from writing "the defect is not one identifier after all, there is a
    second". Reading the spec line killed it: both specs say "IMPLEMENT ALL FOUR METHODS FULLY
    (no stub, no ErrNotImplemented, no panic, no TODO)". The name is FORBIDDEN. Zero declarations
    is PERFECT COMPLIANCE — the sign was inverted, and 0% was the best possible score.
    The tool now classifies FORBIDDEN and excludes those rows, with the reason in the source.
    SEVENTH time in this campaign a hand-built filter produced a confident wrong number, and the
    second time it was caught by reading the underlying case before the number reached a log.

⚠️ WHAT THIS IS NOT
    · NOT a demonstration that the missing use site CAUSES the drop. It is one more property
      unique to one name, and on the archive alone it is exactly as untestable as the other four.
    · The archive cannot separate it from length either: no long name in the corpus HAS a use
      site, and no short name LACKS one. The natural control (taskflow ErrNotFound, 2 mentions)
      is 11 characters, not 20.
    · The rates pool over each project's spec variants — a tree does not record which variant
      drew it. The tool prints DISAGREE and declines to join where variants differ.
    · Pools are small outside ledger and taskflow (2 and 3 trees). The 100%s there are weak
      individually; the row that matters is ledger's 27 and taskflow's 13.

⭐ WHY IT IS WORTH MORE THAN THE OTHER FOUR ANYWAY: IT IS THE ONLY ONE THAT CAN BE MANIPULATED
  WITHOUT TOUCHING THE IDENTIFIER
    Length, compoundness, plausible-abbreviation and domain-package are all fixed by the choice
    of name. A use site is not. Two directed experiments exist and neither substitutes a name:

        ADD one to ErrInsufficientFunds  — same 20 characters, same spelling, one clause added.
        REMOVE the use sites of a name that HAS them — and see whether a short, unambiguous,
        100%-of-27 name starts to fail. This one escapes the n=1 trap completely, because the
        prediction is made about a DIFFERENT identifier.

    The second is the stronger test and it is the one queued: strip ErrExists (9 chars, 27/27)
    down to ErrInsufficientFunds' exact profile — listed in models.go, mapped in response.go,
    demanded nowhere — WITHOUT editing models.go's own purpose by a single byte, and read what
    models.go declares. Prereg follows.

IT ALSO MECHANISES THE CONSUMER/DECLARER ASYMMETRY RATHER THAN RESTATING IT
    31 July: response.go references models.ErrInsufficientFunds 25/25 while models.go declares it
    9/25, and the log corrected itself to say the asymmetry is carried by one name, not by roles.
    This says why the one name can do that. The SAME two mentions are a code instruction for the
    consumer ("map this error to 422" — emit the token) and a list item for the declarer. The
    consumer has a job that requires the exact string; the declarer has a list. The file that is
    supposed to OWN the name is the only one never given a reason to write it.
