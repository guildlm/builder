⚠️⚠️ BUT IT IS NOT THE FLOOR, AND SAYING SO IS THE WHOLE POINT OF THIS FILE
`xserver` is a CROSS-PROCESS pair (chain6 on 4439 vs xserver on 2212). Every arm above is a
WITHIN-process pair. Comparing them directly assumes the two kinds of drift are the same size,
which is the open question, not an answer. The within-process floor draw is running now.

    And even when it lands it will be a 2212 floor applied to 4439 arms, taken on a server up
    minutes with a cold cache rather than hours with a warm one. Pre-committed at 09:52: that
    number establishes THAT a floor exists, not HOW BIG it was for these arms.

WHAT IS ALREADY SAFE TO SAY, FLOOR OR NO FLOOR
The three NULL arms are the most robust rows in the table. They score CODE 1 / 0 functions — the
minimum a landed edit can score — and they do it while a zero-edit cross-process draw scores 4/3.
Whatever the floor turns out to be, a null measured BELOW an untouched redraw is not an artefact
of noise; noise does not push results toward zero.

    The direction of the doubt is asymmetric and it lands entirely on the PERTURBED column.
    That is the reverse of how the series has been reading itself: "everything else perturbed,
    6 of 6" was the strong claim and the nulls were the fragile ones.
