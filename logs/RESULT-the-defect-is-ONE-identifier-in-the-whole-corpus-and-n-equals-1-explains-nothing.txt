RESULT — 5 of 6 spec-required sentinels are declared 100% of the time. The sixth is 36%.
==========================================================================================
31 July, 21:15, while pair 2's control generates. Specs and archived snapshots only, no GPU.

THE MEASUREMENT
    Every sentinel any spec's purpose names, scored against every archived tree that declares
    sentinels at all:

        sentinel                    len   declared   trees    rate
        ErrInsufficientFunds         20          9      25     36%
        ErrUnbalanced                13         25      25    100%
        ErrValidation                13         13      13    100%
        ErrNotFound                  11         43      43    100%
        ErrInvalid                   10         30      30    100%
        ErrExists                     9         43      43    100%

    FIVE OF SIX ARE PERFECT, across 13 to 43 trees each. The model declares the exact name the spec
    asks for, every time, across a month of harness versions and at least three server processes.

    THE ENTIRE DEFECT IS ONE IDENTIFIER. Restricted to ledger, where all five appear together:
    ErrInvalid, ErrNotFound, ErrExists and ErrUnbalanced are 25/25 exact; ErrInsufficientFunds is
    9/25, abbreviated to ErrInsufficient 9 times and omitted entirely 7 times.

    That is not general non-compliance and it is not sloppiness. It is one name.

⚠️⚠️ AND n=1 ON THE TREATMENT SIDE, SO NO EXPLANATION IS TESTABLE HERE
    The obvious reading is length: 20 characters against a next-longest of 13, and the substituted
    name is the first 15 of them. But there is exactly ONE name of 16+ characters in the entire
    corpus, and it is the only failing one, so "long names fail" and "this name fails" are the same
    observation wearing two labels.

        names >= 16 chars   mean rate  36%   n = 1
        names <  16 chars   mean rate 100%   n = 5

    Every property unique to this identifier explains the data equally well:
        · it is the longest
        · it is the only TWO-CONCEPT compound (Insufficient + Funds)
        · it is the only one with a natural shorter form that reads as complete —
          `ErrInsufficient` is a plausible Go name; `ErrNot` and `ErrUn` are not
        · it is the only one whose domain (money) has its own package in these specs

    I CANNOT SEPARATE THEM AND I AM NOT GOING TO GUESS. The honest statement is the localisation,
    not the cause.

    ⚠️ A TEST EXISTS AND IT IS CHEAP: add a second long sentinel to a spec — say
    `ErrDuplicateTransaction` — and draw. If it also degrades, length or compounding is implicated;
    if it does not, the defect belongs to this name specifically. That is a spec change plus one
    draw, and it is recorded rather than run because the harness and specs are frozen under the
    cross-process experiment.

WHY THE LOCALISATION IS WORTH HAVING ANYWAY
    IT BOUNDS EVERY DOWNSTREAM CLAIM MADE TODAY. The cross-package sentinel defect, the 6%-of-builds
    failure rate, ctl-1's green-to-red flip, P3's confirmed mismatch — all of them trace to this one
    identifier. None of them is evidence of a broad naming problem, and the record should not read
    as though it were.

    IT ALSO EXPLAINS WHY THE CONSUMER IS PERFECT. response.go references five sentinels and gets all
    five right, 26 of 26. It is not that consumers are more careful than declarers in general — four
    of the five names are trivially right for everyone. The asymmetry is real but it is carried
    entirely by one name, and the earlier result overstated it by describing "the declarer" and "the
    consumer" as if the difference were about roles.

    ⚠️ THAT IS A CORRECTION TO logs/RESULT-the-consumer-is-more-faithful-to-the-spec-than-the-
    declarer-25-of-25.txt. Its numbers stand; its framing generalised from one identifier to a claim
    about declarers versus consumers, and this measurement does not support that.

WHAT SURVIVES UNCHANGED
    The spec-phrasing fix still targets this correctly — backticking at the declaration site would
    put ErrInsufficientFunds into _required_decls, and the gate would reject a candidate that
    abbreviates or omits it. The fix does not depend on knowing WHY the model abbreviates.
