FINDING — the error that cost today's draw is rare, and the loop usually recovers from it
==========================================================================================
31 July. Log-only, no corpus reads (the parity A/B is writing into generated/).

WHAT KILLED THE DRAW
`generated/ledger-gatefix-1` exhausted 6 fix rounds (5 budgeted) and stopped on a repeating error
surface, all of it one line:

    vet: internal/api/transactions_test.go:28:75: multiple-value json.Marshal(t1)
         (value of type ([]byte, error)) in single-value context

A textbook small-model Go error — a two-value call used where one value is expected. The harness
carries a shelf of deterministic fixers (_fix_errors_wrap, _fix_struct_literal_key, _fix_atomic_inc,
_fix_unused_var, _requalify_stdlib) and has none for this shape, so the obvious move is to write one.

THE POPULATION SAYS OTHERWISE
    651 builds segmented across the archive · 161 ended failing
    27 builds show "multiple-value X(...)" anywhere · of those, 4 ended failing

    So the shape appears in 4% of builds and the fix loop RECOVERS FROM IT 23 TIMES IN 27. It is not
    a systemic blocker; today's draw is one of the four.

    The call sites are spread, which is the other half of the argument against a fixer:
        16  s.Totals        9  Decode        1  s.store.ListProjects        1  json.Marshal

    json.Marshal — the one that killed today's draw — appears in exactly ONE build in the whole
    archive. A deterministic fixer written from today's failure would be written for an n of 1.

CONCLUSION: NOT WORTH BUILDING, on these numbers
    A fixer here would address a shape the loop already resolves 85% of the time, and the specific
    instance that motivated it has occurred once. The right response to today's failed draw is to
    note it and move on, not to grow the deterministic shelf by one more pattern.

⚠️ THE POINT OF WRITING THIS DOWN is that "the fix loop burned six rounds on one line" is a
compelling story, and I went looking for the fixer before I went looking for the frequency. The
frequency took one command and killed the idea. Same shape as the stripper decision earlier today:
plausible mechanism, tiny population, don't build it.
