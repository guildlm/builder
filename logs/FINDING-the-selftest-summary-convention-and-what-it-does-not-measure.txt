FINDING — 11 of 31 self-tests SAY what they leave alone; that is a fact about summaries, not coverage
=====================================================================================================
31 July. A small result, and a second discarded stronger one, in the same session as
logs/FINDING-a-prose-classifier-that-failed-its-own-ground-truth.txt — and discarded the same way.

WHAT I WANTED TO MEASURE
    Five of today's six instrument defects were caught by a case the instrument had to FAIL to flag.
    So: how many of the repo's self-tested tools actually carry such a case?

WHAT IS ACTUALLY MEASURABLE FROM THE SUITE
    `_selftest_all.sh` prints each tool's own one-line summary. Eleven of the 31 that ran under
    --fast describe BOTH sides — "flags the door nobody opens, SILENT on the ones tests use",
    "repeats flagged, progress SPARED", "reports present-but-unnamed only, IGNORES subtests".

    That is a real and useful CONVENTION: a summary that names what the tool leaves alone tells the
    next reader what it cannot catch. Eleven tools follow it.

WHAT I NEARLY REPORTED INSTEAD
    "20 self-tests have no negative case." That claim is FALSE and I checked before writing it,
    against a tool whose tests I wrote myself this morning:

        _repair_take_audit.py — summary is bare "self-test: OK", so the classifier put it in the
        20. Its self-test contains FOUR negative cases: a qualified mention (http.Handler) claimed
        by neither extractor, an unexported name claimed by neither, prose emphasis rejected by the
        MixedCaps guard, and a symbol the donor was also promised being correctly refused.

    The classifier was reading SUMMARY STRINGS and I was about to read it as TEST COVERAGE. Same
    error as the entry-order classifier an hour ago: a regex over prose, no ground truth, and a
    number that would have been quoted.

SO THE MEASURED CLAIM IS THE NARROW ONE
    11 of 31 tools' summaries name a negative case. Nothing here measures how many tools HAVE one,
    and the two are not the same question. Measuring the real one means reading 31 self-tests, which
    is a job, not a grep.

    ⚠️ The convention is worth spreading on its own terms — `_repair_take_audit.py` would be better
    if its summary said "qualified mentions and unexported names are not claimed" instead of "OK",
    because the summary is what appears in the suite output that gets cited.
