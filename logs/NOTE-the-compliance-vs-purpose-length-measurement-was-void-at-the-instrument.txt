THE HYPOTHESIS
    The previous result observed that models.go's purpose is 1212 chars with 11 backticked items and
    complies 36% of the time, while response.go's is 471 chars with 2 items and complies 25 of 25.
    It flagged "longer purposes are complied with less" as the obvious next hypothesis AND EXPLICITLY
    REFUSED TO CALL IT A FINDING at n=2. This was the attempt to test it properly.

WHAT I BUILT AND WHAT IT PRODUCED
    demand  = MixedCaps identifiers inside backticks (the spec language's explicit marker)
    compliance = |demand ∩ (top_level_decls | method_decls)| / |demand|

        purpose length   pairs   mean compliance          demands   pairs   mean compliance
        400-799            102        51%                 2-4          81        41%
        800-1199             8        40%                 5-9          50        60%
        1200+               71        40%                 10-19        50        39%

    Length looked weakly monotonic. Demand count did NOT — 41% then 60% then 39% — which is what
    stopped me from writing it up.

THE INSTRUMENT IS VOID, and one look at the extraction shows why
    ledger models.go's backticked chunks, and what my extractor pulled from each:

        `Account{ID string, Name string}`            -> Account, Name
        `Posting{AccountID string, Amount money.Money}` -> Posting, AccountID, Amount, Money
        `Transaction{ID string, Memo string, ...}`   -> Transaction, Memo, Postings, Posting
        `func (a Account) Validate() error`          -> Account, Validate
        `total.Add(...)`                             -> Add
        `var total money.Money`                      -> Money

    Of those: Account, Posting, Transaction and Validate are things this file declares. Name,
    AccountID, Amount, Memo and Postings are STRUCT FIELDS. Money and Add belong to ANOTHER PACKAGE
    (money.Money). None of the latter can ever appear in top_level_decls, so they are counted as
    failures by construction.

    THE 40-50% "COMPLIANCE" IS THE FIELD-AND-FOREIGN-NAME FRACTION, not a property of the model.
    Both tables measure my regex. They are void and are not to be quoted.

WHY I AM NOT FIXING THE EXTRACTOR AND RE-RUNNING
    A correct demand set would have to recognise struct-literal type names, func and method
    signatures, and var/const declarations — while excluding fields and foreign qualified names.
    THAT IS EXACTLY _required_decls, and exactly the problem measured earlier today: the shipped
    extractor is 95% precise BECAUSE it keys on declaration syntax and refuses to guess, and every
    widening collapsed precision (logs/RESULT-widen-the-spec-not-the-extractor-93pct-precision-for-
    zero-code.txt).

    So "what does this purpose demand" is not a cheap sub-step of the compliance question — it IS
    the hard problem. The hypothesis is not testable by this route at this cost, and that is the
    honest closure rather than leaving it dangling as future work.

    ⚠️ A ROUTE THAT WOULD WORK: score compliance only against demands _required_decls already
    extracts, where precision is known. That measures a narrow, real subset — but the subset is
    biased toward exactly the syntax-explicit purposes, which is plausibly correlated with the thing
    being tested. Recorded as a design note, not attempted.
