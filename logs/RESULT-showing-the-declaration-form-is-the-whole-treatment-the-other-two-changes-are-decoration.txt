⚠️⚠️ THE CLAIM IS "SHOWING THE FORM REPAIRS IT", NOT "BARE LISTS BREAK IT" — AND THE EVIDENCE
AGAINST THE SECOND READING WAS ALREADY IN THE RECORD BEFORE TODAY

The tempting headline is "a bare list of names causes omitted declarations". It is wrong, and
31 July already measured why. In the SAME bare-list sentence of the SAME purpose:

    ErrUnbalanced   25/25   100%        ErrNotFound   43/43   100%
    ErrValidation   13/13   100%        ErrInvalid    30/30   100%
    ErrExists       43/43   100%        ErrInsufficientFunds   9/25   36%   <- the only failure

Four sentinels sit in that bare list and are declared perfectly, in the same files, by the same
processes, across a month of harness versions. Bare-list phrasing is therefore NOT sufficient to
cause the defect. What today's arms establish is narrower and it is a claim about the REPAIR:
where the defect occurs, showing the declaration form removes it, and neither of the other two
edits is needed to do so.

⚠️ AND THAT MAKES THE MECHANISM STILL UNNAMED. Why one identifier among six fails under phrasing
that four siblings survive is the question 31 July left open — length, two-concept compound,
having a plausible-looking abbreviation, or the name itself — and n=1 identifier still cannot
separate those. This result does not touch that question. It says what fixes the symptom, not
what causes it.

WHAT THIS IS GOOD FOR
A narrow, portable, zero-code rule for writing specs:
    SHOW THE DECLARATION FORM. `var ErrX` beats ErrX in a list of names.
Not "use more words", not "explain the initialiser", not "say exported" — those were arms A and
C and they bought nothing measurable.
