RESULT — FIX 2 handles all three stubs generated today, case by case
====================================================================
31 July 16:29. A verification the pre-registration could not have arranged: the parity experiment
produced three fresh instances of the exact defect FIX 2 addresses, on trees that did not exist when
the widening was designed.

    $ _repair_take_audit.py --specs ledger-parity --cases

    NARROW  6 trees · 3 with an empty planned .go file · take non-empty 0 · take EMPTY 3
    WIDE    6 trees · 3 with an empty planned .go file · take non-empty 3 · take EMPTY 0

    EMPTY -> MOVABLE  ledger-parity-arm-1  internal/store/memory.go  wants=[MemStore, Store] take=[MemStore]
    EMPTY -> MOVABLE  ledger-parity-arm-2  internal/store/memory.go  wants=[MemStore, Store] take=[MemStore]
    EMPTY -> MOVABLE  ledger-parity-arm-3  internal/store/memory.go  wants=[MemStore, Store] take=[MemStore]

    Three of three. Under the OLD extractor all three are blocked — `wants` collapses to {Store},
    which store.go legitimately owns, so `take` is empty and the repair no-ops silently. Under the
    widened one, `MemStore` becomes visible in the backticked purpose and the repair has a symbol and
    a donor.

WHY THIS IS A BETTER TEST THAN THE CORPUS IT WAS SCORED ON
    The 125-tree baseline FIX 2 was measured against consisted of trees that already existed. Any
    regex tuned against them risks fitting them. These three were generated hours after the widening
    shipped, by an experiment about a prompt clause that has nothing to do with the extractor, and
    the fix was never adjusted afterwards.

    ⚠️ AND THE THREE ARE NOT INDEPENDENT. arm-1, arm-2 and arm-3 are byte-identical as drawn, so this
    is one stub confirmed three times, not three stubs. The strength here is that the case is NEW,
    not that there are three of it.

    Which is the honest form of the claim: FIX 2 was shown to handle a defect instance it was not
    designed against. Once.
