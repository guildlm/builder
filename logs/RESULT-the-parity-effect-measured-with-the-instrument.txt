WHAT THE INSTRUMENT ADDS OVER "SPLIT vs CONSOLIDATED"
    1. THE EDIT LANDED. Withholding 543 characters from store.go's prompt changed store.go. That is
       not trivial — a null edit that changed only collateral would mean the manipulation missed.
    2. THE BLAST RADIUS IS TWO FILES, both in the same package, and 16 of 19 files are untouched
       across conditions. The effect is local, not a different program.
    3. THE FUNCTION-LEVEL COLLATERAL IS TWO TEST FUNCTIONS. memory.go's seven functions are
       "ctl-1-only" — they exist in the control and not in the arm, which is the stub — so the tool
       reports zero CHANGED functions there and names the asymmetry instead of scoring it as churn.

    ⚠️ Point 3 is the one I could not have produced by hand and nearly got wrong in the opposite
    direction: a naive function diff would call seven functions "changed" when they are seven
    functions PRESENT ON ONE SIDE. That distinction is exactly what the 21:40 note on 30 July said
    the FUNCTION-LEVEL COLLATERAL column exists to make.

WHAT THE THREE DRAWS PER CONDITION DID BUY, and it is not nothing
    · a demonstration that generation is deterministic within a process, which is what exposed my
      repaired-vs-unrepaired error when the numbers refused to line up
    · three independent chances for the parity verdict to disagree with itself; it did not
    · out-of-sample instances of the defect FIX 2 addresses, which the extractor then handled

    None of those was the reason for running three pairs. They are consolation, not justification,
    and calling them the justification afterwards is exactly the move a pre-registration exists to
    prevent.

    ⚠️ THE CORRECTED DESIGN ALREADY FIXES THIS: 1 pair × 3 PROCESSES is the same six draws with the
    replication crossing the boundary that actually varies. Same cost, real evidence. The lesson is
    not "run fewer draws" — it is "replicate over the thing that varies", and the cost of getting
    that wrong is measured here at 3.5 hours.
