FINDING — a prohibition holds, unless you also supply the answer
================================================================
2026-07-29. Two specs, one night, the same edit shape, opposite outcomes — and the
difference is a single property of the justification.

PROVISIONAL: taskapi-v5's router_test.go is written but the draw has not finished, so a fix
round could still touch it. Confirmed status follows when the draw lands.

THE PAIR

  shortener — prohibition PLUS the correct value:
      "The redirect request is a SETUP STEP. Do NOT assert anything about it — not its
       status, not its Location header, not its body."
      ...and in the paragraph explaining why: "...expected something other than 301, and 301
      is exactly what this service's redirect returns..."

      RESULT: it asserted anyway. `if rec2.Code != http.StatusMovedPermanently`. The
      prohibition was dropped and the VALUE was used.

  taskapi — prohibition ALONE, no value:
      "assert the STATUS CODE is 200, and assert NOTHING ELSE. Do NOT touch the body at all."
      The entry never says what the body contains.

      RESULT: no body assertion. Just the status check.
          func TestHealthz(t *testing.T) {
              ... if rec.Code != http.StatusOK { t.Errorf(...) }
          }

THE RULE, and it is sharper than what I had

I had drafted two rules tonight that looked like they conflicted: "for an option you do not
want exercised, WITHDRAW it — explaining is not a constraint" (from healthz going wrong
twice), and "the prohibition was ignored; the VALUE is what fixed the draw" (from shortener).
Both were measured. Neither is the rule.

    A prohibition WORKS. It stops working the moment the same entry also supplies the
    answer, because then there is something to use, and using it is easier than obeying.

That is actionable in a way neither draft was: when you forbid an assertion, the
justification must argue WITHOUT stating the correct value. Explaining WHY the assertion is
wrong is fine. Saying what the right one would be defeats the sentence it is attached to.

It also re-reads the original healthz failure. The wording that failed twice was "if you
check it at all, decode it into a `string`, never into a struct" — which is not merely an
optional assertion, it is an optional assertion WITH THE METHOD SUPPLIED. Same defect: the
sentence handed over the means while gesturing at restraint.

WHAT THIS DOES NOT SHOW

n=1 per side, on two specs with different histories, and shortener is the noisiest artifact in
the corpus. What makes it worth writing down anyway is that the prediction was recorded in
logs/PENDING-verifications.txt BEFORE the draw, with both outcomes and what each would mean:
"No body assertion => withdrawal works when unaccompanied by the answer. A correct body
assertion => what fixes these is information, never prohibition." The first branch is what
happened.
