RESULT — in-budget repeats: 176 rounds spent, and 15 builds that went green anyway
====================================================================================
31 July, while the arm-4 verification draw holds the GPU. Zero GPU, archive only. Found by
following a question the arm-4 prediction correction raised rather than by planning it.

THE POLICY UNDER TEST
    The extension guard stops the fix loop when an error surface repeats — but only past the flat
    budget:

        if rnd > max_fix_rounds and sig in seen_surfaces: break

    A repeat inside the budget is logged ("surface seen before") and deliberately NOT acted on, so
    the corpus stays measured under a single behaviour. That decision is documented in builder.py
    and has never been costed with the current signature.

MEASURED across logs/ with `_error_signature` as it stands after today's timestamp normalisation
        logs with 2+ reconstructable fix rounds        145      <- the denominator
        ...that ever repeat a surface                   96      (66%)
        ...whose FIRST repeat lands inside the budget   95      <- the policy's cases
        rounds spent after a first repeat, total       176
        median rounds after a first repeat               2
        builds that went GREEN despite a repeat          15     (16%)

    So acting on in-budget repeats — stopping the loop the moment a surface recurs — would buy back
    176 rounds and would have KILLED 15 builds that converged.

    THE WORST OFFENDER IS ONE OF THE FIFTEEN. iso-shortener-07161437 repeated at round 2, ran
    seven more rounds, and went green. Under a stop-on-repeat policy it would have died at round 2
    with the most rounds-after-repeat in the archive.

    THE POLICY IS THEREFORE DEFENSIBLE ON THIS EVIDENCE, which is not what I expected to find and
    is the opposite of what the first version of this measurement said.

⚠️⚠️ THE FIRST VERSION SAID ZERO, AND IT WAS THE INSTRUMENT
    The green detector was `re.compile(r"green|all checks passed|BUILD OK", re.I)` applied to
    `text.rsplit("\n", 40)[-1]`. Both halves are wrong, in opposite directions:

    FALSE NEGATIVE — `rsplit("\n", 40)[-1]` is the LAST LINE, not the last 40 lines. A green
        build's final line is `Generated ledger into ./generated/x`; the marker
        `converged to green after fix round 1` sits two lines above it. Checked against
        ledger-parity-ctl-1, which exited rc=0: last-line match FALSE, whole-text match TRUE.

    FALSE POSITIVE — the bare word `green` matches FIX 1'S OWN WARNING, "build never went green".
        A build that explicitly announces it never converged reads as converged. That warning is
        the newest line in this repo, added today, and it is in 3 logs.

    Fixed to the builder's single success marker, `^\[guildlm-build\] converged to green`, and
    both traps are now assertions in the self-test.

        broken detector    0 of 96 went green   ->  "the policy never pays"
        fixed detector    15 of 96 went green   ->  "the policy pays 16% of the time"

    ⚠️ A ZERO IS A CLAIM ABOUT THE INSTRUMENT BEFORE IT IS A CLAIM ABOUT THE WORLD. This one was
    caught because the number was surprising enough to check, which is not a method — the check
    should not depend on the answer being interesting. What made it checkable in one command was
    having a KNOWN-LABELLED case to hand: ctl-1 exited rc=0, so any detector that calls it not-green
    is broken regardless of what the aggregate says.

WHAT THIS DOES NOT SAY
    NOT a claim about the timestamp fix. That fix acts past the budget, where the loop already
    stops on repeats; it makes the stop FIRE (arm-1: 8 rounds -> 6). This is a different policy
    question that the same reading of the loop happened to expose.

    NOT comparable to the 27 in-budget repeats counted on 29 July. That count used the error-LINE-SET
    signature; this uses the whole-output fingerprint plus today's normalisation. Third instrument,
    third set of numbers, and builder.py already warns that the two share a name and are not the
    same object.

    NOT a full accounting of cost. "176 rounds" is rounds, not minutes: today's own durations showed
    two arms doing IDENTICAL work 5.2 minutes apart, so round counts are the stable unit and any
    minutes attached to them are an average dressed as a measurement.

    AND THE 145 DENOMINATOR IS RECONSTRUCTED, not authoritative. It counts logs where two or more
    fix rounds could be rebuilt from the `!`-prefixed lines the builder prints. A build whose output
    was captured differently is invisible here.

⚠️⚠️ CORRECTED — "15 GREEN BUILDS SAVED" WAS 13 MERGED LOG FILES. THE REAL NUMBER IS ONE.
════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════
A LOG FILE IS NOT A BUILD, and this tool read one file as one build.

    log files                                 781
    ...holding more than one build             40      (FULL-SUITE.log holds 19)
    build segments once split                1174

CAUGHT BY A CONTRADICTION, not by suspicion: a "build" reported FOUR failed rounds AND
"converged to green after fix round 1". Only a merged log can produce that. iso-shortener-07170020
is four separate builds, each failing once with the same error and each converging — read as one
build, it became a build repeating a surface four times and recovering. Thirteen of the fifteen
"saves" were that artifact.

⚠️ AND THE FIRST FIX FOR IT WAS ALSO WRONG. Splitting on the build's opening line,
`generate <file> (1/N)`, left iso-shortener-07170020 merged — that log format never prints it. The
tool reported the same wrong sequence with a segmenter that looked like it worked. Segmentation is
now TERMINATOR-based, and a build ends unambiguously in both outcomes:

    success   "Generated <name> into <path>"    (a failed build never prints this: arm-1 and
                                                 arm-4 both 0, checked)
    failure   "exhausted N fix rounds"

RE-MEASURED PER BUILD SEGMENT
        build segments with 2+ fix rounds                129     (was "145 logs")
        ...that ever repeat a surface                     88
        ...whose first repeat lands inside the budget     88
        rounds spent after a first repeat                156     (was 176)
        builds that went GREEN despite a repeat             1     (was 15)

    THE ONE SURVIVOR: bitset-witness-07271826#1 — 2 rounds, sequence A A, converged at round 2.
    It saw the same surface twice and the second round's fix worked.

    SO THE EARLIER CONCLUSION IS WITHDRAWN. "The policy pays 16% of the time" was 13 merged files
    and 1 real case. It pays 1 time in 88.

AND THAT MAKES A CONCRETE POLICY CHANGE FREE — measured, not argued
════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════
Simulating "stop after K CONSECUTIVE identical surfaces" against every archived trajectory.
Stopping can only TRUNCATE a trajectory, never redirect it, so a green build is lost only if it
actually reached K consecutive repeats:

        K=2   fires on 81 builds   saves 151 rounds   kills 1 green   <- the survivor above
        K=3   fires on 59 builds   saves  83 rounds   kills 0 green   <- FREE
        K=4   fires on 52 builds   saves  31 rounds   kills 0 green

    NO GREEN BUILD IN THIS ARCHIVE EVER REACHED THREE CONSECUTIVE IDENTICAL SURFACES. So a
    3-consecutive stop recovers 83 of the 156 wasted rounds at zero measured cost, and it does not
    touch the flat budget or the extension rule — it is a separate, narrower condition.

    ⚠️ NOT APPLIED. builder.py is frozen under the running cross-process parity experiment, and a
    change to the fix loop mid-run would split that experiment across two harnesses — which is
    what today's freeze guard exists to prevent. Queued.

    ⚠️ AND IT IS A RETROSPECTIVE SIMULATION. Truncation is sound (it cannot invent trajectories),
    but every archived build ran under the CURRENT policy; a build that changes behaviour because
    it stopped earlier — the repair pass now runs sooner, and since this afternoon the repair
    actually does something — is not modelled here. The zero is a zero on historical trajectories.
