AMENDMENT 2 — written while the hunt is running and BEFORE any eligible process is found
16 August. This is a bookkeeping decision about the PROBE BUDGET, made at the only honest moment:
before I know whether the budget will bind, and before any arm of the new series exists.

THE SITUATION. The machine's GPU watchdog is killing mlx_lm.server mid-draw
(kIOGPUCommandBufferCallbackErrorImpactingInteractivity). Three of the first four baseline probes
of the ax2- series died that way, one after passing the baseline gate and dying on the screen.

THE DECISION. A probe that ends VOID-SERVER-DIED **classifies no process** — it is not a
discarded LONG, it is not a discarded ABBREVIATED, it is an attempt that produced no verdict at
all. The registered budget ("max 10 probes") is a budget on CLASSIFYING baseline probes, and void
attempts do not consume it. The ledger records every attempt either way; the two counts are
reported separately in the result log, so a reader can apply whichever rule they prefer.

⚠️ WHY THIS IS NOT A LICENCE TO PROBE FOREVER: the discarded-probe count is what makes the
campaign's central claim CONDITIONAL ("among processes whose declarer gets the name wrong..."),
and a void attempt carries none of that information because it never reached a declarer. The
conditional claim is unaffected by how many times the GPU died. The effort cap stands at 10
classifying baseline probes and is not being raised.

⚠️ THE HUNT SCRIPT AS RUN TONIGHT COUNTS ALL ATTEMPTS, including void ones, against its loop
limit — i.e. it is STRICTER than this amendment, not looser. It was not edited mid-run, because
bash reads a script lazily and editing a running one is how a protocol silently becomes a
different protocol halfway through. If it exhausts, the relaunch will count classifying probes
only, and the result log will say which rule produced the process that hosted the arms.
