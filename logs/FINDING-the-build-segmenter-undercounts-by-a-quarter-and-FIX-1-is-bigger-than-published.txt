FINDING — the archive holds 853 builds, not 654, and FIX 1's population is 60% larger than published
======================================================================================================
31 July, while the cross-process parity run holds the GPU. Logs only, no corpus reads, no GPU.
Followed from today's discovery that a log file is not a build.

THE DISCREPANCY THAT STARTED IT
    Two instruments in this repo disagree about how many builds the archive contains:

        published findings (29-31 July)   651 / 654 builds
        _repeat_cost.py after today's fix  853 completed builds

    Both segment logs. They disagree by a quarter, and the smaller number is the denominator of at
    least three published percentages.

WHY THE OLDER COUNT IS LOW, measured rather than assumed
    logs/RESULT-how-big-the-silent-population-was.txt segments at "generate <file> (1/N)" — the
    build's OPENING line. Counting markers across all 781 log files:

        'generate X (1/N)'          build STARTS       658
        'Generated X into'          build SUCCEEDED    596
        'exhausted N fix rounds'    build FAILED       257
        outcomes total                                 853

        logs with OUTCOMES but NO start marker         103
        build outcomes inside them                     160   (79 green, 81 failing)
        logs with a start but no outcome                35   (killed, or still running)

    103 log files record builds that ran to completion and never print the opening line. Every build
    in them is INVISIBLE to a start-based segmenter — not miscounted, absent.

    ⚠️ AND THAT RESULT PREDICTED THIS FAILURE IN ITS OWN TEXT. Its closing note says the segmentation
    "has a known edge: the final segment of a multi-build log runs to EOF BECAUSE NOTHING MARKS A
    BUILD'S END." Something does: a build ends at "Generated X into" or "exhausted N fix rounds", and
    a failed build prints the former zero times (checked on arm-1 and arm-4). The limitation was
    correctly identified and the fix for it was one regex away.

RE-COUNTED, terminator-segmented
                              published    corrected
        green                      454          596
        exhausted-and-failing      160          257      <- FIX 1's population, +97 builds (+61%)
        completed                  614          853

    TWO INDEPENDENT METHODS AGREE ON 853: summing per-segment outcomes gives 596 + 257, and counting
    the raw outcome markers across whole files gives 596 + 257. That agreement is the check that
    makes this number worth more than the one it replaces.

    ⚠️ "INDETERMINATE" IS NOT COMPARABLE AND I AM NOT REPORTING IT AS A COUNT. Terminator
    segmentation produces a trailing segment for whatever follows the last outcome — usually a suite's
    RESULT summary, not a build. That inflates indeterminate from 36 to 177 and it is segmentation
    debris. The trustworthy figures are the two with outcome markers in them.

WHAT CHANGES, AND WHAT DOES NOT

    FIX 1 IS BIGGER THAN PUBLISHED, NOT SMALLER. "160 failing builds, every one silent about whether
    the project it gave up on matched its own plan" is really 257. The change I shipped this morning
    addresses 61% more of the archive than the result claimed for it.

    THE LOAD-BEARING CLAIM SURVIVES A 60% LARGER DENOMINATOR, which is the real test:

        green builds carrying an EMPTY-file warning     11  ->  19
        FAILING builds carrying one                      0  ->   0

    Zero, still, across 257 failing builds instead of 160. Before FIX 1 the warning was structurally
    unreachable on a failing exit, so zero is the number the mechanism predicts — and a structural
    claim that holds on a denominator 60% larger is better evidenced than it was.

    THE "1% CARRY A TIMESTAMPED APP LOG LINE" FIGURE BARELY MOVES. 9 of 654 is 1.4%; 9 of 853 is 1.1%.
    The conclusion that drove the timestamp fix — the defect is real, live and narrow — is unaffected.

⚠️ THE PATTERN, WHICH IS THE POINT OF FILING THIS
    Every published number in this campaign that is a RATE has a segmenter under it, and the
    segmenter was never itself measured. It was checked once against an expectation ("zero failing
    builds carry a warning — that is what the mechanism predicts, so the segmentation is trustworthy")
    and that check PASSED for both segmenters, because the property being checked is insensitive to
    the 160 builds one of them cannot see.

    A CHECK THAT PASSES ON A BROKEN INSTRUMENT IS NOT VALIDATION. What separated them here was
    counting the same thing two independent ways and requiring the answers to match — which costs one
    extra query and would have caught this a week ago.
