NOTE — a cross-spec order measurement, computed and discarded in four minutes
=============================================================================
31 July 17:00. The fourth measurement today thrown away, and the second-to-last one caught BEFORE it
was written up rather than after.

THE QUESTION
    Today established that ledger splits correctly with the implementer entry first and consolidates
    with the interface first. Does the order effect hold on the other specs? The corpus has 350+
    trees; the join looked trivial — read each tree's spec, note whether store.go precedes memory.go,
    check whether store.go declares MemStore.

WHAT IT RETURNED
        spec        entry order    split   CONSOLIDATED
        ledger      iface-first        5             1
        ledger      impl-first        12            10
        taskapi     iface-first       12             7
        taskapipro  iface-first       32            12
        workapi     impl-first        13            42

    Which says ledger CONSOLIDATES LESS with the interface first, and workapi consolidates MOST with
    the implementer first — the reverse of everything measured today, on both specs at once.

WHY IT IS INVALID, AND IT IS NOT SUBTLE
    `spec_for(tree)` returns TODAY's spec. The entry order in specs/ledger.yaml was CHANGED on
    30 July at 22:51 ("promote the order fix: implementation entries now precede the interfaces they
    satisfy").

        ledger-v4          drawn 26 July      under iface-first
        ledger-name1       drawn 30 July 12:09 under iface-first
        ledger-pairA-p6    drawn 30 July 19:42 under iface-first
        ledger-parity-ctl-1 drawn 31 July      under impl-first

    Every ledger tree drawn before 22:51 on 30 July is being labelled with an order it was never
    drawn under. The table is not measuring the order effect; it is measuring when the spec changed.

    ⚠️ SAME CLASS AS THE AFTERNOON'S RETRACTION: attributing a CURRENT state to an artifact produced
    under a DIFFERENT one. There it was repaired-vs-unrepaired; here it is today's spec vs the spec
    of the day. The corpus does not record which spec version produced a tree, so this join cannot be
    fixed by being more careful — it needs data that was never captured.

WHAT WOULD ANSWER THE QUESTION
    Draws made deliberately, both orders, same process, same spec text otherwise — which is what the
    pairA/pairB series did for ledger on 30 July and what the corrected parity design does for one
    clause. A retrospective sweep over trees drawn across a spec edit cannot substitute for it.

    ⚠️ AND THE RESULT THAT LOOKED MOST INTERESTING IS THE ONE THAT SHOULD HAVE WARNED ME. A table
    contradicting two separately-measured facts at once is far more likely to be a broken join than a
    discovery, and noticing that took less time than computing it.
