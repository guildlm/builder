I. THE CAMPAIGN'S CAPSTONE
--------------------------
    314 v4 rows · 307 v5 rows · 295 comparable · 140 LIVE IN BOTH
    CAUGHT -> SURVIVED    2      (published as 3; one was an ordinal artefact)
    SURVIVED -> CAUGHT    6      (published as 12; six were ordinal artefacts)
    177 of 314 rows dead — five artifacts do not compile

REDRAW DRIFT IS WORTH ABOUT 6 UP AND 2 DOWN at this corpus size. That is the number the
campaign never had, and it is the baseline every future closure claim must beat. Ten of the
twelve originally-reported gains sat in artifacts nobody edited, so the gains were never
attributable to closures even before the artefact correction.

⚠️ 56% of the baseline was unanswerable. Whatever the flips say, the repaired specs produced
five red trees out of twenty-three.

⚠️ AND OF THE 8 SURVIVING FLIPS, ONLY 2 HAVE CLEAN PROVENANCE ON BOTH SIDES (16:55). Five have
an as-drawn v5 tree and a v4 tree whose build log cannot be identified — 11 of the v4 baseline
trees were restored wholesale and carry one uniform mtime. One (usersapi) is unattributable
outright: that draw's single model rewrite was of a test file, and its only flip is a defence
gained. Six-up-two-down stands as the best available estimate; it rests on two fully controlled
rows out of 25 fully controlled live rows (of 140 total: 18% clean-both, 62% partial, 20%
mismatched). The dominant uncertainty is NOT model rewrites — taskflow and tasks-api carry 73
of the 140 rows and both have a CLEAN v5 side and an undatable v4 tree. And the flip rate is
not higher where provenance is dirty (8.0% clean / 5.7% partial / 3.6% mismatched), which
rules out a large inflation effect at counts far too small to separate. See
RESULT-the-capstone-flips-by-provenance.txt.

    So the original test-side failure got two attempts out of seven, and four rounds went to

⚠️ THE cannot-FAIL / cannot-PASS TRIGGER IS NOT ESTABLISHED. It read:
    cannot-FAIL (asserts 1)  passes silently, ships, artifact survives   <- chain4
    cannot-PASS (asserts 2)  fails loudly, triggers widening, tree dies  <- chain5
The two shapes are real and the asymmetry between them is real. What is NOT established is
that a redraw chose between them. chain5's round 1 failed to COMPILE, so its twins were first
observed in round 2 — after that file had taken a deterministic fix, a drained-body rebuild
and a model rewrite. chain4 took only a deterministic fix and converged. (1,1) versus (1,2) is
therefore not a comparison of two draws, and chain5's as-drawn values are not recoverable from
any log. A within-draw round-1 rewrite explains the difference at least as well.

A REPEATED ERROR SURFACE IS A PERFECT PREDICTOR: 127 detections, 0 recoveries. The builder
already computes it and only acts past the flat budget. The four-word fix is NOT licensed by
that number — it was measured with a different signature — and logging was added so the next
corpus can answer it properly.
