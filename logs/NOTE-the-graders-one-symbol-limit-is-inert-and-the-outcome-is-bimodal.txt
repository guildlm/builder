NOTE — the grader's one-symbol limit changes no verdict, because the outcome is all-or-nothing
==============================================================================================
31 July. Tests the caveat recorded an hour ago against every graded tree, rather than leaving it as
a stated risk.

THE CAVEAT
    `_parity_ab.sh` grades SPLIT vs CONSOLIDATED by asking whether store.go declares MemStore. A
    store.go that took the SENTINELS but not MemStore would grade SPLIT while still violating its
    purpose, "The Store INTERFACE only — no implementation in this file".

THE TEST — a strict check beside the grader on all 11 ledger pair/parity trees
    strict = does store.go declare EXACTLY {Store} and nothing else

    grader SPLIT        (5 trees)  store.go declares ['Store']                       strict: pass
    grader CONSOLIDATED (6 trees)  store.go declares 9 to 11 symbols                 strict: fail

    AGREEMENT: 11 of 11. The one-symbol grader and the declaration-only check never disagree on any
    tree this campaign has produced.

AND THE REASON IS THE INTERESTING PART: THE OUTCOME IS BIMODAL
    Every SPLIT store.go declares exactly one symbol. Every CONSOLIDATED one declares nine to eleven
    — the interface, MemStore, its constructor, all six methods, and on four of them the sentinels
    too. There is no middle: not one tree took the sentinels and left MemStore, or took the
    constructor and left the methods.

    So the failure mode is not "store.go drifts past its purpose by degrees". It is a switch between
    two whole shapes: a declaration file, or a complete working package. That is worth knowing before
    designing any finer-grained measure of it — a scale would have nothing to measure.

⚠️ THE CAVEAT STANDS AS A CAVEAT. Nothing here says a partial over-reach cannot happen; it says it has
not happened in 11 trees, all of one spec. The grader is safe to keep using and unsafe to generalise
from, which is the same sentence as before the test — only now with a number attached.
