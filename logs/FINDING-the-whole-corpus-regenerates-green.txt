FINDING — all twenty specs regenerated GREEN on the base model, first pass
===========================================================================
2026-07-26. An unplanned experiment: I deleted the corpus, so every -v4 tree had to be
rebuilt, and rebuilding it ran the whole spec suite end to end in one session.

THE RESULT
      20 specs · 20 GREEN · 0 NOT-GREEN     (build + vet + test -race, verified
                                             independently of the builder's own verdict)
      0 escalations                          base 7B only — no fleet member was configured
      15 of 20 needed NO fix round at all    5 converged after fix round 1
      total wall clock ~64 minutes           41s (demo-small) to 889s (taskflow)

  The configuration was the ordinary one: mlx-community/Qwen2.5-Coder-7B-Instruct-4bit at
  temperature default, --candidates 2, retrieval few-shot with 2 shots, --max-fix-rounds 5.

WHY THIS IS WORTH RECORDING
  taskflow and tasks-api were written down as CAPABILITY WALLS as recently as yesterday —
  3381 and 3100 seconds, eleven and nine fix rounds, ending RED. Both are in the list above
  at 889s and 412s, green, and neither needed more than one fix round. Nothing about the
  model changed. What changed was two spec sentences and one gate.

WHAT IT DOES NOT SAY
  GREEN IS NOT DEFENDED. That is the whole teeth programme: a suite can pass while the
  invariants it names go unchecked, and the same afternoon that produced this table also
  produced a mutation showing taskflow's ListProjects sort is undefended in exactly this
  tree. Twenty green artifacts is a statement about the BUILD loop, not about the tests.

  ONE DRAW EACH. Twenty runs is one sample per spec, not a distribution. `-count=4` removes
  in-run map-order flake but says nothing about run-to-run variance, and this corpus has
  measured 25%-of-runs failures before.

  THE SPECS ARE NOT THE ONES THAT FAILED. Several were edited this week precisely because
  they failed — the nextID sentence, the TestListLimit code block, the named Content-Type
  tests. This is the suite as it stands today, not a rerun of the runs that were red.

WHAT IT COSTS
  The pre-deletion corpus was a MIX: 44 archived failures, thirteen BASELINE-RED rows, two
  artifacts that did not compile at all. Those were not clutter — they were the only inputs
  exercising the "a mutation on an already-red artifact is VOID" rule, and the substrate for
  gate mining. A corpus where everything is green has nothing to mine and nothing to void,
  which is why generated/plantedred-v4 had to be planted by hand and why the flat _fail-*
  archives are a real loss (FINDING-i-deleted-the-corpus.txt).

  It is also the confound the redraw diff has to carry: rows that read BASELINE-RED before
  will read CAUGHT or SURVIVED now because there is finally a suite to ask, and that is an
  artifact changing state, not a defence changing. _redraw_diff.py counts those on their own
  line for that reason.
