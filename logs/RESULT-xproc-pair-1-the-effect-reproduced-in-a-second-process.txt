⚠️ AND THE ENDPOINT'S OWN FILE BEHAVES DIFFERENTLY IN THE TWO CONDITIONS
        control   internal/store/store.go   377 vs 382 chars   PROSE — 0 of 0 funcs differ
        arm       internal/store/store.go  2263 vs 2233 chars   CODE  — 2 of 7 funcs differ
                                                                (CreateAccount, GetAccount)

    In the CONTROL the endpoint's subject is stable across processes: comments and strings only,
    while seven other files were rewritten. In the ARM it is not — store.go there holds the whole
    consolidated package, and two of its methods changed.

    That asymmetry is expected once stated — 382 bytes of interface has little to vary and 2.2KB of
    implementation has plenty — but it matters for how much this design can ever prove. The
    experiment asks whether the CLAUSE flips store.go's content; in the arm condition, the process
    flips part of it too. The VERDICT is robust to that (a file either declares MemStore or does
    not) which is exactly why the endpoint was defined as a binary and not as a diff.

CONVERGENCE — recorded, not an endpoint

        parity arm-1   exhausted 8 fix rounds
        xproc  ctl-1   exhausted 6 fix rounds

    In the old process the control converged and the arm did not. In the new process NEITHER did.

    THE CONTROL'S FAILURE HAS A KNOWN CAUSE and it is not the manipulation: models.go declared
    `ErrInsufficient` where response.go wrote `ErrInsufficientFunds`, and no fix round could
    reconcile them (logs/FINDING-the-cross-package-API-block-drops-every-grouped-sentinel-6pct-of-
    builds.txt). That is a defect of the harness and the spec phrasing, independent of the parity
    clause, and it happens to have landed in this control.

    ⚠️ WHICH IS A REASON TO WATCH PAIRS 2 AND 3 RATHER THAN CONCLUDE ANYTHING. If their controls also
    fail on the same sentinel, the cross-process control condition is being sampled with a known
    confound sitting inside it — one that does not touch the SPLIT/CONSOLIDATED endpoint, but does
    make "the control went green" unavailable as a sanity check.

    Both arms stopping at 6 rather than 8 is the timestamp fix working, as arm-4 already established.
