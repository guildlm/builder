FINDING — the "capability limit" is a 1-in-3 coin flip, and the answer was already on disk
==========================================================================================
30 July, 12:55. Zero GPU. Found by looking at nine ledger trees that already existed instead of
drawing a tenth.

THE CLAIM BEING TESTED WAS MY OWN, FROM 45 MINUTES EARLIER
At 12:49 I wrote that ledger's ErrInsufficient defect "was a coin flip, not a capability wall".
The evidence was n=2: one control that failed and one arm that succeeded. That is not enough to
call anything a coin flip, and it was my claim, so it is the one to test.

I was about to spend a ~35 minute draw to get n=3. The answer was on disk at n=9.

THE COUNT — every ledger tree in generated/
    ErrInsufficientFunds (what the spec asks for)   6   ARM-A · ARM-B · ARM-C · BEFORE-specfix
                                                        · prose1 · v4
    ErrInsufficient      (the defect)               3   ctl-proc3 · name1 · v5

    internal/api/response.go uses ErrInsufficientFunds in ALL NINE. The consumer never varies.
    The entire defect is models.go's declaration coming out short, and it does so 3 times in 9.

    So the model writes the required name TWO TIMES IN THREE. The wall is a 33% miss rate.

THE REQUIREMENT HAS BEEN STABLE — checked, not assumed
Every revision of specs/ledger.yaml back to 15 July asks for ErrInsufficientFunds (x2 or x4;
the count changed, the NAME never did). So all nine trees were drawn against the same requirement
and the comparison is legitimate.

    ⚠️ zsh silently broke the first version of this check: `git show $rev:specs/ledger.yaml` parses
    the colon as a variable modifier, so every revision reported x0 and it looked like the spec had
    NEVER required the name. Braces fixed it. A check that returns all-zeros is not evidence of
    absence — it is a check to re-run.

⚠️ WHAT WEAKENS IT, STATED PLAINLY
Only THREE of the nine trees carry .pre-fix.json (ctl-proc3, name1, prose1). The other six are
POST-REPAIR, so in principle a fix round could have corrected the name after the draw, which would
inflate the 6.

    Against that: the one direct test of whether the fix loop CAN repair this symbol is
    ctl-proc3, which ran SIX rounds — three of them taking models.go — and never corrected it.
    A repair mechanism that fails six times out of six on the file in question is unlikely to be
    the reason the other six trees look correct. And for all three snapshotted trees, the as-drawn
    name equals the on-disk name.

    So: 6-of-9 is the honest number, with the caveat that 6 of the 9 are unverifiable as-drawn and
    the number could in principle be as low as... nothing, actually — the three DEFECTIVE trees are
    all snapshot-verified. The floor on the defect rate is solid; only the ceiling is soft.

WHAT IT CORRECTS
The campaign wrote this defect up as a capability limit: "the LONGEST name got SHORTENED and was
never repaired in six rounds", cited as evidence about what a 7B can hold. That framing came from
draws that happened to miss. The model gets it right two times in three; what six fix rounds
cannot do is REPAIR it once it has been written, which is a completely different claim about a
completely different part of the pipeline.

    ⚠️ AND THE SECOND CLAIM SURVIVES INTACT, which is why the distinction matters rather than being
    a quibble. "The fix loop cannot repair a wrong sentinel name" is supported: 6 rounds, 3 visits
    to the file, no correction. "The model cannot write the right sentinel name" is refuted at 2/3.

⚠️ THE IRONY IS EXACT AND WORTH KEEPING. This file's own method note says the cheapest instrument
