PREREG — does 6 August's shipped repair survive on a process that passes a placebo screen?
==========================================================================================
10 August, committed BEFORE any arm. Follows 54afc12, which found that the whole 5–6 August
sentinel series lacks a content-free control, and that one of two processes probed that day would
have produced its exact results for any edit at all.

THE CLAIM UNDER TEST (mine, shipped)
    "Show the declaration form in specs — `var ErrX` beats a bare name list." Its evidence is
    base/base2 ABSENT vs treated/treated2 LONG on two processes, plus a varonly arm isolating one
    of three components. Every one of those arms edits models.go's purpose. None of them edits it
    WITHOUT the sentinel change, so none can separate the treatment from the perturbation.

THE INSTRUMENT
    specs/ledger-ownplacebo.yaml — models.go's own purpose, +54 folded characters against the
    treatment's +51 (a 3-character mismatch, printed by the builder), in the same style (backticks
    around identifiers, a short parenthetical), naming NO sentinel, with the sentinel line
    surviving verbatim — all asserted in the builder's self-test and by _spec_pair_guard.py.

PROTOCOL — THE SCREEN IS A GATE, NOT AN ARM
    1. Probe a fresh process with the baseline spec. Discard unless INFORMATIVE (ABSENT or
       ABBREVIATED). Max 6 probes, every one recorded.
    2. SCREEN it with ledger-ownplacebo.yaml, RESTART=0.
         · flips to ef8c15d6  -> the process cannot discriminate. DISCARD IT, record the screen
           verdict in PROBE-LEDGER.txt, and go back to step 1. It is not eligible for a treated
           arm and no treated arm may be run on it "just to see".
         · stays 3a78b0d8    -> eligible.
    3. On an eligible process only: run specs/ledger-origorder.yaml (the SHIPPED treated spec),
       then the baseline again to close.

WHAT EACH OUTCOME MEANS, WRITTEN DOWN NOW
    TREATED FLIPS ON AN ELIGIBLE PROCESS — the shipped rule survives its first real control, and
        6 August's conclusion is upgraded from "confounded" to "supported on n=1 controlled
        process". It does NOT become a general claim.
    TREATED DOES NOT FLIP ON AN ELIGIBLE PROCESS — the shipped rule is in serious trouble: on the
        only process where the question is answerable, the treatment does nothing. The 6 August
        log must then be marked SUPERSEDED and the "zero-code shippable rule" withdrawn from the
        save point until it is re-earned.
    NO ELIGIBLE PROCESS IN 6 PROBES — the 6 August design is not merely uncontrolled, it may be
        UNCONTROLLABLE by this method, and that is itself the result. Recorded, not worked around.
