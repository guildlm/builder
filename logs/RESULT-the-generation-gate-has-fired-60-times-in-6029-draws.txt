WHAT THIS DOES TO THE QUEUED SPEC FIX'S EXPECTED VALUE
    The fix makes the gate demand ErrInsufficientFunds. Two things follow, and they point opposite
    ways:

    IN ITS FAVOUR — the gate is nowhere near saturated. 60 rejections in 6029 draws means there is
    no cost story about over-rejection from the current baseline; the simulated widening (+144
    correct demands at 93% precision) would multiply the gate's activity several-fold from a very
    low base.

    AGAINST IT — a rejection is only useful if the RESAMPLE differs, and the archive says candidate
    2 is chosen 9 times in 6029. That is not evidence that candidate 2 is IDENTICAL — it is chosen
    only when candidate 1 fails, which is itself rare — but it means the resample path is almost
    entirely untested by this corpus. Combined with this evening's finding that the model
    abbreviates despite seeing the name three times, the honest expectation is that a rejected
    candidate may well abbreviate again and the loop falls back after 2 tries.

    ⚠️ WHICH IS EXACTLY WHY THE PRE-REGISTERED ENDPOINT IS "THE GATE FIRES" AND NOT "THE TREE IS
    CORRECT". That wording was chosen this evening on a different argument; this measurement is a
    second, independent reason for it. If the endpoint had been the tree, the fix could look like a
    failure while doing precisely what it was designed to do.

    AND IT SUGGESTS A CHEAP COMPANION: raise `--candidates` for the file that fails. 30 fallbacks in
    6029 events means the budget is almost never exhausted, so more candidates cost almost nothing
    on average and only spend where the gate is actually struggling. Recorded, not proposed — it
    changes generation cost and needs its own pre-registration.

⚠️ WHAT THE 0.5% DOES NOT MEAN
    IT IS NOT "THE MODEL IS RIGHT 99.5% OF THE TIME". It is the rate at which a candidate satisfies
    the checks the gate ACTUALLY PERFORMS — parse, stdlib-only, no cross-file redeclaration, and the
    thin slice of promised declarations _required_decls can see. Today established that the last of
    those misses two thirds of what the purposes promise. A gate that rarely fires is evidence about
    the gate before it is evidence about the model, which is the same lesson as this morning's
    "a gate that never failed isn't a gate".
