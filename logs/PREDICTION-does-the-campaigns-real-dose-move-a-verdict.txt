PREDICTION — at the campaign's REAL dose, does spec growth move a mutation verdict?
====================================================================================

20:58 on 29 July, before the draws. The last open question in this thread, and the one the
20:53 result named: jsoncodec got TWELVE lines and moved no verdict, while the specs whose
growth coincided with the capstone's flips grew 43 to 73.

THE DESIGN
    artifact   ratelimit — spec NEVER edited by the campaign (0 net lines), 5 live rows
               across FOUR distinct mutation shapes, 7 spec files. The most varied row set
               among the untouched specs; jsoncodec's six rows were all one shape in one file.
    dose       48 lines: the SAME tracked twelve-line inert block, inserted into FOUR entries.
               That is squarely in the campaign's 43-73 range, and spreading it across entries
               mirrors how the campaign actually grew specs — closures added in several places,
               not one paragraph in one entry.
    placement  the four entries are bucket.go, registry.go, main.go and ratelimit_test.go.
               NOT api.go and NOT middleware.go — the two files that carry every measured row.
               So the added lines describe no file with a verdict, and any verdict change is
               unambiguously COLLATERAL.
    control    ratelimit redrawn unmodified on the same server, back to back. ratelimit-v5 was
               drawn 00:43, before process 4439 started, so it cannot serve as the control.

BRANCHES
  A  >=1 of the 5 verdicts differs between arms
     -> spec growth at the campaign's real dose moves mutation verdicts. The capstone's
        6-up/2-down is, in substantial part, an artefact of the campaign growing its own specs,
        and the 20:53 null was a dose artefact.
  B  no verdict differs, but api.go or middleware.go differ in CODE
     -> growth reaches the files that carry verdicts and still does not move them. Combined
        with jsoncodec, that is two doses and two artifacts saying perturbation rarely
        propagates to an invariant, which substantially rescues the capstone's headline.
  C  no CODE difference anywhere
     -> the effect does not reproduce at this dose or on this spec, which would contradict
        four prior measurements and should be treated as a reason to distrust this arm rather
        than as a result.

PREDICT  A 35% · B 50% · C 15%.
  A is plausible because four times the dose across four entries is a much larger intervention
  than anything tested, and because ratelimit's rows include status-code and header-drop
  shapes that live in exactly the kind of handler code the perturbation has been rewriting.
  B is the single most likely outcome because a verdict only moves if the perturbation lands
  on the specific line a mutation targets, and the added lines describe neither file.
  C is low but not negligible: ratelimit converged in 0 fix rounds in its v5 draw, and a spec
  that lands first-try green may be one the model has a very settled shape for.

⚠️ WHAT I WILL NOT DO WITH BRANCH B. It is the outcome I expect and the one that most helps
the campaign's headline, and that is exactly why it needs stating in advance: B at one more
dose and one more artifact is still TWO data points. It bounds the worry; it does not close
it, and it must not be written up as though the capstone were vindicated.
