1. THE FLOOR DOES NOT EXIST, AND THE GAP IS WHERE IT DIES
    +15 reaches LONG. +17 does not. +19 does not. +20 does, twice, in two different wordings.
    No threshold in size can produce that ordering. Region count is held at two, region identity
    is held, the construction is held, the name list is byte-identical in every arm, and the
    ordering is still non-monotone. Registered P(monotone) = 0.35 — deliberately low, and still
    too generous. This is the FOURTH non-monotonicity in the campaign and the first one drawn
    inside a range chosen specifically to contain a boundary.

2. ONE CHARACTER OF SIZE, IN THE SAME SLOT, DECIDES THE OUTCOME
    L6   +14  "all readily"    ABSENT
    F1   +15  "all reliably"   LONG
    Same slot, same construction, same everything else in the file, one character apart in
    rendered size — and they land on opposite ends of the three-valued scale. Both reproduced:
    L6 is ABSENT on four screened processes now, F1 redrew LONG byte-identical.

    Set that beside the two nulls this campaign already had: +54 characters of content-free
    editing ONE SENTENCE ABOVE does nothing, and +19 IN THIS VERY SLOT does nothing. The
    instrument is not insensitive and it is not size-driven. It is arbitrary at the token level.

3. TWO ARMS AT IDENTICAL SIZE BOTH REACH LONG — AND WRITE DIFFERENT FILES
    f-F4 (+20, "all quite readily") and f-paraphrase (+20, "every one of them") are the same
    size, in the same two regions, and both LONG — but ef8c15d6 and 649bdef7 respectively. The
    prereg's primary branch (F4 LONG AND paraphrase LONG, registered 0.65 conditional) HIT, and
    it is the strongest version the size/location account has had. It also confirms 10 August's
    correction from a new direction: REACHING LONG IS NOT WRITING THE SAME FILE. The endpoint is
    the declaration; byte-identity is a bonus that is not always available.

4. THE POOLED TALLY SURVIVES INTACT WHILE THE STRUCTURE IT SUGGESTED IS REFUTED
    Recomputed over four screened processes, 51 ledger rows:

        location    size              n     reached LONG    above ABSENT
        on-line     >= +20           10      10 of 10        10 of 10
        on-line     +15..+19          5       2 of 5          2 of 5
        on-line     <= +14           15       2 of 15         5 of 15
        off-line    >= +20            7       0 of 7          2 of 7

    Every number in that table is consistent with "bigger edits flip more often" AND with "no
    edit size is a threshold". Size predicts the RATE at which an arbitrary rewrite lands; it
    does not predict any individual rewrite, and the 8-of-8 that motivated this experiment was
    a rate misread as a boundary. A monotone-looking pooled tally and within-process
    non-monotonicity are not in conflict, and this is the cleanest demonstration of that the
    campaign has produced.

    ⚠️ The off-line row is unchanged and still says what it said on 11 August at three-valued
    resolution: 0 of 7 reach LONG, but 2 of 7 are above ABSENT. Off-line edits are not inert.

WHAT SURVIVES, STATED AT THE RESOLUTION IT WAS MEASURED
    · LOCALISATION, three-valued: only ON-LINE edits ever reach LONG (14 of 30 on-line, 17 of 30
      above ABSENT; 0 of 7 off-line reach LONG, 2 of 7 above ABSENT), while off-line edits can
      still move a process one step.
      📌 I first typed "12 of 30" here from memory. The buckets say 14. The tally tool now prints
      per-location totals for exactly this reason — a summary sentence is where a hand-added
      number goes unchallenged, and that is the eighth hand-made counting error of the campaign,
      the third caught before publication.
    · SIZE AS A RATE, not a rule: rewrite the declaration line by ~+20 and 10 of 10 pooled arms
      have landed; but +15 has landed and +19 has not, so no individual promise can be made.
    · DETERMINISM WITHIN A PROCESS: 12 draws, three byte-identical baselines at start, middle and
      end, both redraws byte-identical. Nothing here is noise.

WHAT THIS COSTS THE SHIPPED RULE
    Nothing new is retracted today. The 6 August rule ("show the declaration form") was already
    demoted on 10 August to a member of a family, and 11 August showed the family is about WHERE
    the edit is, not what it says. Today prices the family: membership is not conferred by size,
    and two adjacent members of the same construction, one character apart, disagree.

PRACTICAL — the strongest form this lesson has had
    A prompt or spec rule derived from ONE A/B is unreliable AT ONE CHARACTER OF RESOLUTION in a
    slot you are deliberately holding fixed. Not at the level of "different phrasing", not at the
    level of "shorter or longer" — at one character. If a wording is going to be shipped, its
    NEIGHBOURS have to be drawn, and the neighbours have to include ones that differ trivially.
    Where a choice must be made without that budget, prefer the large rewrite: it is the only
    region of this space where every arm ever drawn has landed.
