FINDING — the stub is the MAJORITY outcome, not three unlucky trees
==================================================================
30 July, 17:05. Zero GPU. 107 trees already on disk, none of them ever asked this question.

WHAT THE 29 JULY FINDING SAID
"Three v5 trees — ledger, taskapipro and workapi — have internal/store/memory.go at FOURTEEN BYTES,
`package store` and a newline, while the spec entry describes a goroutine-safe Store implementation
with eight methods. The implementation was written into store.go instead." Written up as a discovery
about three artifacts, found by chance while chasing a relocated mutation site.

THE CLAIM
The stub is not an edge case. It is the majority outcome for workapi (80%) and ledger (67%), and
roughly three in five draws overall. A defect written up as "three trees" is a coin flip the
campaign has been drawing on all month.

⚠️ WHAT WEAKENS IT
Only TWO of the 107 are as-drawn snapshots (ledger-prose2, taskapipro-preedit); the rest are
post-repair. Normally that would sink the measurement.

    It does not here, and the reason is the original finding's OWN central point: "a stub compiles,
    vets and tests clean... it is invisible to every other instrument here." A fix loop only runs on
    failures, so it never has a reason to touch a stub, and it cannot create one either. Post-repair
    state therefore preserves this property in both directions. That is an argument, not a
    measurement — but it is the argument the 29 July finding itself established.

⚠️ AND THE ONE DIRECT TEST AGREES: ledger-prose2's as-drawn snapshot is the stub, and its 6 fix
rounds never repaired it — consistent with a loop that cannot see it.

WHY IT MATTERS BEYOND ITS OWN ROW
    1. Every measurement keyed on (artifact, FILE) silently stops joining when the implementation
       relocates. The 29 July note said so; nobody then knew it applied to over half the corpus.
    2. It dissolves open question #3 twice over. That question asked "server or day?" for the stub.
       Today's arm 4 produced a stub from an inert edit on the same process that had produced five
       non-stub trees, and now the corpus says the outcome is near coin-flip across a month of
       draws on many servers and many days. There was never a stable cause to find.
    3. It re-frames what "the spec describes memory.go's contents" buys. Nothing, 58% of the time.
