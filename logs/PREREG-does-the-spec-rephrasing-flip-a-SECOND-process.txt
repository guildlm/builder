PREREG — does the spec rephrasing flip the declarer on a SECOND server process?
================================================================================
Written 6 August, BEFORE the treated arm of process #2 runs. The baseline arm (probe p2-base)
was already in flight when this was written, and that is deliberate and harmless: a baseline
probe is a SELECTION step, not a test. It classifies a process; it stakes no prediction. The
prediction below is conditional ON that classification and is committed before the arm that
could confirm it exists.

⚠️ THE THING THIS FIXES IS NAMED IN THE RESULT IT EXTENDS.
logs/RESULT-the-spec-phrasing-flipped-the-declarer-on-a-replicated-baseline-and-the-gate-never-fired.txt
ends with its own next-steps list, and item 3 is:

    "Another informative process, probed and paired the same way. Two processes is not many;
     one is fewer."

That result is 4 draws on ONE process (pid 1459). Byte-identical repeats inside a deterministic
process are cheap evidence about that process and NO evidence about another one. 31 July
established that this exact declaration varies BY PROCESS — pid 7598 wrote the long name 7/7,
pid 49288 wrote an abbreviation 2/2, pid 86026 wrote nothing 2/2. So the single most likely way
for yesterday's headline to be wrong is that it is a fact about pid 1459.

THE PROTOCOL — three arms, in this order, on ONE process, no restart between them
    1. p2-base     baseline spec   (SELECTION: LONG -> discard the process, restart, try again)
    2. p2-base2    baseline spec   (CONTROL, run BEFORE the treatment — see below)
    3. p2-treated  treated  spec   (the arm under test)

⚠️ ARM 2 RUNS BEFORE ARM 3, AND THAT IS THE ONE PROCEDURAL CHANGE FROM YESTERDAY.
Yesterday's control2 was run AFTER the treated result was known. That was defensible — a control
can only refute, so a late one costs nothing but its lateness — and it was recorded as a
weakness. It costs ~10 minutes to not have that weakness, so this time the replicated baseline
is established first and the treatment is drawn into a baseline that is already fixed.

    specs/ledger-origorder-baseline.yaml   the phrasing as it was before 8961ed3, restored
                                           BYTE-EXACT from git (`git show 8961ed3^:...`), never
                                           retyped — this campaign has a standing rule about
                                           hand-copied artefacts producing table columns.
    specs/ledger-origorder.yaml            the treated phrasing, live since 8961ed3.

    _spec_pair_guard.py asserts the two differ at internal/models/models.go AND NOWHERE ELSE,
    including file order, because _file_list renders every purpose into every prompt. It also
    fails when the named purpose does NOT differ, which is the silent way to run an experiment
    with no treatment in it. Both directions are self-tested.

THE PREDICTION — three-way, and CONDITIONAL ON THE BASELINE CLASS

Scored three-way (LONG / ABBREVIATED / ABSENT), because on 31 July a binary long-vs-short
question could not express the outcome that actually occurred, and the third branch was already
measured at 28% of the archive when the binary prediction was written.

⚠️ The two informative baseline classes are NOT equally hard, and I am predicting them apart
rather than averaging them into one number. pid 1459 was ABSENT — the model omitted the name.
An ABBREVIATED process has actively CHOSEN a competing name, which is a stiffer disposition to
move: on pid 49288 the fix loop regenerated models.go FIVE times with `undefined:
models.ErrInsufficientFunds` in front of it and got a BYTE-IDENTICAL file every time.

    IF the baseline arm is ABSENT       P(treated = LONG) = 70%    (25% abbreviated, 5% absent)
    IF the baseline arm is ABBREVIATED  P(treated = LONG) = 50%    (45% abbreviated, 5% absent)

WHY NOT HIGHER. The mechanism is clean and direct — the prompt text changed what the model wrote
on the first sample, no gate rejection involved — and that generalises more readily than a
resampling effect would. But process-level variation in this one declaration is the best
established fact in this whole line of work, and it is precisely the thing a second process
tests. Yesterday I put 80% on a pathway I could verify offline and lost; the campaign's own
ledger says confident numbers are 0 of 6 and near-50 numbers are 3 of 4.

REJECT — the honest failure, stated so it cannot be reinterpreted afterwards:
    The treated arm comes back ABBREVIATED or ABSENT. Then the rephrasing does NOT generalise
    across processes, yesterday's result is a fact about pid 1459, and the correct headline
    becomes "one process flipped" rather than "the rephrasing fixes the declarer". I will say
    that in those words.

VOID — the probe answers nothing, rather than answering no:
    - any arm returns NO-FILE (models.go never landed) or VOID-REPAIRED (the fix loop ran, so
      the tree is not as-drawn) or VOID-CLASSIFIER;
    - the server pid CHANGES between arms — the pairing is the design, and a restart between
      arms confounds the treatment with the process it is being compared against. The pid is
      recorded on every ledger row for exactly this check;
    - the baseline arm is LONG. Then no treated arm runs at all: a fix cannot be tested where
      the defect does not occur. The process is discarded, logged, and another is probed.
    - p2-base and p2-base2 DIFFER from each other. Then generation on this process is not
      deterministic and the whole paired design is inapplicable to it. This would itself be a
      finding — every process measured so far has been deterministic within itself — but it
      voids the treatment comparison rather than informing it.

WHAT A CONFIRMATION WOULD AND WOULD NOT BUY
    ✓ Two processes, not one. The claim stops being about pid 1459.
    ✗ It is still CONDITIONAL BY CONSTRUCTION. Both processes were SELECTED for failing the
      baseline; LONG processes are discarded and logged in logs/PROBE-LEDGER.txt. The claim can
      only ever be "among processes whose declarer gets the name wrong, the rephrasing fixes
      it". No rate may be computed from probes — the archive answers that (36/36/28) and the
      probe throws away exactly the evidence a rate needs.
    ✗ Still ONE SPEC (ledger) and still NO BUILD RUN TO GREEN. These draws stop at file 4 of 19
      by design.
    ✗ n=2 is not a sample. Two processes agreeing is two processes agreeing.

⚠️ AND THE TREATMENT IS A BUNDLE, WHICH THIS DESIGN DOES NOT UNPICK AND DOES NOT CLAIM TO.
The one-line edit changed THREE things at once: "Sentinel errors" -> "Exported sentinels", bare
names -> backticked `var ErrX`, and an added "(each errors.New)". Which of those does the work is
a separate question and is NOT asked here.

⚠️ CORRECTED, same day, before the unbundling was designed: I first wrote FOUR things, counting
"a re-wrap that moves where ErrInsufficientFunds sits in the list". Both halves of that are
wrong. The purpose is a FOLDED yaml scalar (`>-`), so it renders to a single line with zero
newlines — the re-wrap is invisible to the prompt. And the name is the fifth and LAST item of
the list in BOTH phrasings, so nothing about its position changed either way. Recorded because
the unbundling experiment would otherwise have spent an arm on a component that does not exist.

ADDENDUM, committed 6 August BEFORE the treated arm runs — an observation that WEAKENS this test
════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════
The baseline arm on the new process (pid 64149) produced a models.go that is BYTE-IDENTICAL to
the baseline arm on pid 1459 — hash 3a78b0d856c4, 1276B, the same file yesterday's experiment
started from.

    pid 1459   baseline   3a78b0d856c4  1276B   ABSENT
    pid 64149  baseline   3a78b0d856c4  1276B   ABSENT   <- a DIFFERENT process, same bytes

⚠️ THIS MAKES THE REPLICATION WEAKER THAN I DESIGNED IT TO BE, AND I AM SAYING SO BEFORE THE
ANSWER EXISTS. The point of a second process was that 31 July established process-to-process
variation in this declaration. A second process that starts from the IDENTICAL failing file is
not the independent test I wanted: if two processes agree byte-for-byte on the baseline prompt,
the most economical reading is that they are in the same numerical regime, and agreeing under
the treated prompt too is much of what "same regime" already means. A flip here is a real second
observation, but it is closer to a repeat than the design intended.

Processes DO still differ — pid 47310 was LONG this morning and is logged as discarded, and
31 July's three processes chose three different names. So the variation is real; these two
particular processes happen to coincide.

I am NOT moving the registered number. 70% stands as written. The honest update is not to the
probability but to what a hit BUYS, and that is stated here rather than discovered afterwards:

    a hit  -> two processes, same baseline file, same treated file. Weaker than "two processes"
              sounds. The stronger version needs a process whose baseline is a DIFFERENT variant
              (the archive has 3 distinct ABSENT files and 2 distinct ABBREVIATED ones), and
              that is the next probe to run, not a substitute for this one.
    a miss -> still decisive in the reject direction. Two processes with identical baselines
              diverging under treatment would mean the treatment's effect is not even a function
              of the process's baseline behaviour, which no current account predicts.
