FINDING — entry order is CONSTANT across all three affected specs, so the corpus cannot test it
==============================================================================================
30 July, 17:45. Zero GPU. Checked immediately after arm 5's decisive files landed, to see whether
the 107-tree corpus could substitute for the multi-draw experiment arm 5 needs.

IT CANNOT, AND THE REASON IS CLEAN
    spec         store.go   memory.go   stub rate (deduped)
    ledger       entry 6    entry 7     67%
    taskapipro   entry 6    entry 7     35%
    workapi      entry 6    entry 7     80%

Order is IDENTICAL in all three: store.go sixth, memory.go seventh, everywhere. So the corpus holds
the variable fixed at one value across 38 distinct draws. It contains no information about order —
not weak information, none — and no amount of re-analysis will extract any.

    ⚠️ THAT IS THE OPPOSITE OF WHAT I WENT LOOKING FOR. The 17:05 and 12:55 results both came from
    asking the corpus a question the campaign thought needed GPU. This time the corpus is structurally
    incapable of answering, and the useful move was finding that out in one command rather than
    building an analysis on top of a constant.

TWO CONSEQUENCES, ONE FOR EACH DIRECTION
    1. ARM 5 IS THE ENTIRE EVIDENCE BASE FOR THE ORDER HYPOTHESIS, at n=1. And its outcome is (a) —
       memory.go full (2374 chars, 7 funcs) when generated first, store.go the interface it is
       supposed to be (377 chars, 0 funcs, declaring Store). Consistent with the mechanism.
       ⚠️ AND I PRE-COMMITTED NOT TO COUNT IT: "(a) is the outcome I expect and the one a 33%
       non-stub base rate hands me for free. Only (c) confirms at n=1." (c) — the stub MOVING to
       store.go — did not happen. So the hypothesis stands where it stood: plausible, specific,
       and untested. It needs N trivially-different variants per order, which is hours of GPU.

    2. THE SPEC-TO-SPEC VARIATION IS NOT ORDER-DRIVEN, and that is a NEW open question. With order
       held constant the rate still ranges 35% -> 80%, a factor of 2.3. Something about these specs
       other than the order of these two entries makes workapi more than twice as likely to stub as
       taskapipro. That cause is unidentified and the corpus CAN speak to it, because unlike order
       it varies.

⚠️ WHAT THIS DOES NOT DO. It does not weaken the 17:15 finding that scope_rule fails 58% of the
time — that is a measured rate against a guard whose text names this exact failure. It only means
the ORDER explanation for why it fails has one supporting draw and no corpus backing.
