FINDING — four of five refusals are the gate reading goroutine IDs as new errors
================================================================================
31 July, found by reading the refusal MESSAGES while the run was still going, rather than by
counting the refusals. Defect is in this morning's fifth edit — my own non-regressing gate.

WHAT THE REFUSALS SAY
    left internal/store/memory.go empty: moving MemStore into it breaks
        : created by testing.(*T).Run in goroutine 20; : goroutine 22 [running]:
    left internal/store/memory.go empty: moving MemStore into it breaks
        : created by testing.(*T).Run in goroutine 34; : goroutine 36 [running]:
    ... two more of the same shape ...
    left internal/store/memory.go empty: moving MemStore into it breaks
        internal/store/memory.go: package models is not in std      <- the only genuine one

    4 FALSE · 1 REAL, of the refusals logged so far.

THE MECHANISM
    `_error_keys` keys any line it cannot parse as `file:line: message` under ("", line). A panicking
    or timing-out test prints a GOROUTINE DUMP, and those lines carry goroutine NUMBERS that differ
    between runs of identical code. So the before-set and the after-set never match, every dump line
    reads as a NEW error, and the gate refuses a move that changed nothing.

    It is the same class of problem `_canonical_toolchain_output` already exists to solve — it strips
    per-package durations, per-test durations and `(cached)`, precisely because they vary run to run
    and carry no information. Goroutine IDs belong on that list and are not on it.

WHY IT MATTERS MORE THAN THE COUNT
    The pre-registered PRIMARY counts red-tree cases the new gate ACCEPTS. Every false refusal is a
    case wrongly excluded, so the measured number is a FLOOR, and the reject condition "no accepted
    move leaves a new (file, message)" is untouched — false refusals are conservative, never unsafe.

    ⚠️ BUT THE RESULT AS IT STANDS UNDERSTATES THE GATE, and I would have reported it as the gate's
    verdict. The refusal count is not a property of the moves; it is partly a property of whether the
    tree's tests happen to panic.

THE FIX, and it is not a widening of the comparison
    Drop goroutine-dump lines in `_error_keys` — the `goroutine N [state]:`, `created by ... in
    goroutine N` and `file.go:NN +0x...` frames — the same way durations are already dropped. NOT by
    loosening the key: a genuinely new panic still shows up as its own error line above the dump, and
    the "package models is not in std" refusal above must keep refusing.

        moving MemStore into it breaks : panic({0x1044529c0?, 0x140000160f0?});

    Frame lines carrying pointer ARGUMENTS. Heap addresses, fresh every run. My filter was built
    from the four messages I had READ, and those happened to show goroutine ids; the same trees
    print different noise on the next run. I fixed the instance, not the class.

WHAT ACTUALLY WORKS — NORMALISE, DO NOT DROP
    `_canonical_error_line` rewrites `0x[0-9a-f]+` to `0xADDR` and `goroutine \d+` to `goroutine N`,
    and every line is then compared. Dropping throws away signal — a genuinely different frame or a
    new panic ought to register — while normalising removes exactly the tokens that vary.

        after: workapi14/15/16/17 -> FILLED     (RED-BEFORE 12 cases: FILLED 11, REFUSED 1)
        and    workapi2           -> REFUSED    "package models is not in std", the one REAL refusal

    That last line is the whole test of it. A noise filter that also silenced the genuine refusal
    would have shown a better number and been worse.

⚠️ SCORING MYSELF: I predicted four flips and got zero, from a fix that passed four tests and a
control check. The tests were right about what I wrote; what I wrote was the wrong generalisation.
Nothing short of running the actual cases would have caught it — and the reason I ran them is that
the pre-registration for this gate names its own reject conditions in terms of CASES, not counts.
